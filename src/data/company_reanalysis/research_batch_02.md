#### Jif Peanut Butter Salmonella Recall — FDA Class I, 16 Sick in 12 States (2022)
- Status: Adjudicated (recall complete; litigation ongoing)
- Date: May 2022
- Details: On May 20, 2022, J.M. Smucker voluntarily recalled Jif peanut butter products (lot codes 1274425–2140425, ending in 425) produced at its Lexington, KY facility after the FDA and CDC linked the plant to a multistate Salmonella Senftenberg outbreak. 16 people were confirmed ill in 12 states; illness onset dates ranged from February 19 to May 2, 2022. The FDA classified the recall as Class I (most serious). Critically, whole-genome sequencing matched the outbreak strain to an environmental sample collected at the same Lexington facility during a 2010 FDA inspection — indicating the contamination source persisted for over a decade. Multiple class action lawsuits were filed immediately, including by Ron Simon & Associates (first filed May 25, 2022). Third-party businesses that used Jif also sued for recall-related losses.
- Source: FDA (fda.gov/safety/major-product-recalls/2022-recalls-food-products-associated-peanut-butter); CDC Investigation Update, May 2022; Top Class Actions, 2022; NPR, 2022

Smucker voluntarily withdrew Gravy Train, Kibbles 'n Bits, Skippy (pet food), and Ol' Roy wet dog food. The FDA informed Smucker in March that the market withdrawal was being reclassified as a formal recall covering all lots manufactured from 2016 through the withdrawal date. - Source: PetfoodIndustry.com; Today.com; Steve Dale Pet World; FDA

#### Kinder Salmonella Outbreak — Multi-Country Recall, Delayed Disclosure (April 2022)
- Status: Adjudicated (recall resolved; regulatory findings remain)
- Date: April 2022
- Details: In April 2022, Ferrero recalled Kinder Surprise, Kinder Happy Moments, and related products across more than 110 countries after the Belgian food agency (FASFC) linked the Arlon, Belgium factory to a monophasic Salmonella Typhimurium outbreak predominantly affecting young children in the UK, France, Germany, Ireland, Sweden, and the Netherlands. Critical finding: FASFC investigations revealed routine samples from December 2021–January 2022 had already tested positive for Salmonella, but Ferrero concluded there was "no risk to the public" and did not notify Belgian authorities — a delayed disclosure that regulators found unacceptable. The Arlon plant was ordered to suspend operations on April 8, 2022, and did not receive final authorization to restart until September 2022. In the US, the FDA issued a voluntary recall notice for Kinder Happy Moments Chocolate Assortment and Kinder Mix Chocolate Treats Basket. A 2025 academic study added further epidemiological detail to the outbreak's scope and causes.
- Source: FDA Recall Notice (fda.gov); Just-Food, 2022; AARP, 2022; HPSC Ireland, 2022; Food Safety News, 2025
- Severity: High
- DB Status: New

#### Hazelnut Supply Chain — Child Labor in Türkiye (Ongoing; 3,020 Children Identified)
- Status: Public Record / Alleged (admitted by Ferrero in sustainability report)
- Date: 2023–2024
- Details: Ferrero's 16th annual Sustainability Report (covering Sept 2023–Aug 2024), released in collaboration with the ILO, identified 3,020 children working on hazelnut farms in Türkiye. Ferrero acknowledged this as a persistent, ongoing issue. The BBC conducted an investigation into the Ferrero/Turkey hazelnut supply chain, documenting child laborers. Ferrero is the dominant buyer of Turkish hazelnuts, a primary ingredient in Nutella and Ferrero Rocher. Since 2021, Ferrero has invested in ILO partnership programs and donated $4M to tackle the issue, but the 2024 report's figure of 3,020 children demonstrates that child labor in its hazelnut supply has not been eliminated.
- Source: Ferrero 2024 Sustainability Report; Logistics Insider (India), 2024; Business & Human Rights Resource Centre; Confectionery Production, 2023

- Date: 2022–2024

Rainforest Action Network (RAN) has published repeated criticisms of Ferrero's palm oil sourcing, noting satellite alerts showing deforestation risk in 3% of supply, and broader concerns about "sustainable" certification not preventing real-world forest loss. Ferrero partnered with Earthworm Foundation in 2021 for its Malaysian palm oil supply chain, directly benefiting 400+ migrant workers.

#### Source of Heavy Metal Contamination (Industry Context)
- Status: Public Record
- Date: 2022–2023
- Details: Research confirms that cadmium in cocoa beans enters naturally from soil and is transported directly by cocoa trees, while lead contamination occurs post-harvest during drying, fermentation, and transport when wet beans are exposed to soil and dust. These are systemic challenges for high-cocoa-content dark chocolate across the industry; the FDA does not currently have enforceable cadmium limits for most chocolate products. Lindt acknowledged that lead and cadmium are "unavoidable in the food supply" and that trace amounts fall below current regulatory limits — but California's Prop 65 MADLs are significantly more stringent than federal FDA standards.
- Source: NPR, 2022; Snopes, 2024; Consumer Reports, 2022
- Severity: Medium
- DB Status: Adds scientific and regulatory context to `lead_lindt`

### Research Findings

#### OSHA Willful Violation — Finger Amputation, $136,532 Penalty (2021)
- Status: Adjudicated
- Date: April 19, 2021 (incident); September 24, 2021 (OSHA citation)
- Details: On April 19, 2021, a 48-year-old worker at Tootsie Roll's Chicago, IL manufacturing plant reached inside an unguarded bag sealing machine (Robert's C-1500 Form, Fill and Seal Machine) to clear paper debris, and the machine's jaws closed, partially amputating the worker's finger. OSHA's investigation found that Tootsie Roll Industries had disabled the safety lock on the machine's access doors so the machine could be operated in bypass mode. OSHA issued one willful serious violation for inadequate machine guarding and proposed $136,532 in penalties. Tootsie Roll disputed the citation and stated intent to meet with OSHA to contest.
- Source: OSHA News Release Region 5 (osha.gov/news/newsreleases/region5/10052021); Bloomberg Law, 2021; Occupational Health & Safety magazine, 2021; Powder & Bulk Solids, 2021
- Severity: High
- DB Status: New

#### Artificial Food Dyes — FDA Phase-Out Pressure (2025)
- Status: Public Record / Regulatory Action
- Date: 2025
- Details: In April 2025, HHS and the FDA announced a national initiative to phase out petroleum-based synthetic food dyes as part of a broader food safety agenda. The Biden administration formally banned Red 3 in January 2025 (effective 2027). Tootsie Roll products contain Red 40, Yellow 5, and Yellow 6 — all petroleum-derived synthetic dyes that remain on the FDA's phase-out target list. CSPI's Synthetic Dyes Corporate Commitment Tracker does not list Tootsie Roll as having committed to removing synthetic dyes. Multiple state laws passed in 2024–2025 ban synthetic dyes in school foods, with California's School Food Safety Act signed in 2024. At Tootsie Roll's scale (small revenue, limited R&D), reformulation poses significant business risk.
- Source: Morgan Lewis, 2026; The Hill, 2025; CSPI Corporate Commitment Tracker (cspi.org); FDA, 2025
- Severity: Medium
- DB Status: Updates `artificial_colors`; adds FDA regulatory action and state bans

#### Opacity / Corporate Governance Concerns
- Status: Public Record
- Date: Ongoing through 2025
- Details: Tootsie Roll Industries is known in food industry circles for extreme corporate opacity. The company rarely grants press interviews, publishes minimal sustainability reporting, and has no public commitments on supply chain labor, environmental impact, or ingredient reformulation. The founding Gordon family retains control and the company has an unusual dual-class share structure. This structural opacity compounds consumer transparency concerns — there is no public record of Tootsie Roll conducting or publishing any supply chain audits.
- Source: Tootsie Roll Industries SEC filings (10-K, 8-K); general industry knowledge
- Severity: Low
- DB Status: New
