## Keurig Dr Pepper Inc. (id: keurig-dr-pepper)

### Research Findings

#### Peñafiel Bottled Water Arsenic Recall — FDA Known Since 2013
- Status: Adjudicated (recall completed)
- Date: 2019
- Details: KDP voluntarily withdrew all unflavored Peñafiel mineral spring water imported from Mexico after independent lab tests detected arsenic at nearly double the FDA limit of 10 ppb. Consumer Reports revealed the FDA had known about elevated arsenic levels in Peñafiel as early as 2013 — six years before KDP acted. Enhanced filtration was subsequently installed at the Peñafiel facility.
- Source: FDA Safety Alert, June 2019; Consumer Reports, 2019 (https://www.consumerreports.org/arsenic-in-food/fda-has-known-of-high-arsenic-levels-in-keurig-dr-pepper-penafiel-bottled-mineral-water/)
- Severity: High
- DB Status: New

#### SEC Settlement — Misleading K-Cup Recyclability Disclosures
- Status: Adjudicated
- Date: 2024 (settled September 10, 2024)
- Details: The SEC charged KDP with making incomplete and inaccurate statements in its 2019 and 2020 annual reports claiming its K-Cup pods "can be effectively recycled" — without disclosing that two large recycling companies had told the company they would not accept pods. KDP paid a $1.5 million civil penalty. The SEC found KDP had conducted recycling-facility tests since at least 2016 that showed pods were not commercially viable for curbside recycling.
- Source: SEC Enforcement, September 2024; Crowell & Moring / Waste Dive (https://www.wastedive.com/news/keurig-dr-pepper-sec-coffee-pod-recyclability-curbside/726745/)
- Severity: High
- DB Status: New (updates existing "pod_waste" entry — the SEC finding confirms greenwashing, upgrades severity)

- Status: Pending (class certification denied, appeal ongoing)
- Date: 2014–2025
- Details: Multiple class-action antitrust cases were consolidated into MDL proceedings in 2014, alleging Keurig used exclusive agreements with coffee suppliers and technological locks on its machines to suppress competition. Direct purchasers (including McLane, BJ's, Winn-Dixie) collectively claimed over $5 billion in damages. In 2025, the SDNY denied class certification; plaintiffs have appealed to the Second Circuit.
- Source: Panabee News; Globe and Mail, 2025 (https://www.theglobeandmail.com/investing/markets/stocks/KDP/pressreleases/36271771/keurig-dr-pepper-appeals-antitrust-class-certification-denial/)

- Date: 2025–2026
- Details: KDP recalled approximately 80,640 K-Cup pods (960 cartons of 84 pods) labeled as McCafé Premium Roast Decaf that may have contained caffeinated coffee. The FDA classified it as a Class II recall on January 23, 2026. Distributed in California, Indiana, and Nevada. Presents risk to consumers with caffeine sensitivities or medical conditions requiring caffeine avoidance.

## Red Bull GmbH (id: red-bull)

### Currently in DB

### Research Findings

#### False Advertising Class Action Settlement — "Gives You Wings"
- Status: Adjudicated
- Date: 2014 (settlement finalized)
- Details: Red Bull paid $13 million to settle a US class-action lawsuit alleging it falsely advertised that its drinks enhance performance, concentration, and reaction speed. Consumers who purchased Red Bull between 2002 and 2014 were eligible for $10 cash or $15 in product vouchers. The court found that Red Bull could not produce credible scientific studies supporting its performance superiority claims over cheaper caffeine sources. Red Bull's 8.4oz can contains roughly half the caffeine of a 7oz cup of coffee.
- Source: CBC News, 2014; Class Action 24, 2014 (https://classaction24.com/red-bull-lawsuit/)
- Severity: Medium
- DB Status: New

#### UK Advertising Standards Authority Ban — Health Claims
- Status: Adjudicated
- Date: 2019
- Details: The UK Advertising Standards Authority banned a Red Bull poster campaign for implying improved focus and concentration — health claims not authorized under EU nutrition and health-claims regulations. This reflects a pattern of regulatory pushback on energy drink health messaging in Europe.
- Source: LawHubX; Law Fold (https://lawfold.com/red-bull-gives-you-wings-lawsuit/)
- Severity: Low
- DB Status: New

#### UK Government Under-16 Sales Ban Announcement
- Status: Pending legislation
- Date: September 2025
- Details: The UK government announced plans to ban retail sales of high-caffeine energy drinks (including Red Bull) to children under 16 in England, citing health risks including heart palpitations, sleep disorders, and behavioral issues in adolescents. This follows similar bans already in place in Wales and Scotland, and reflects growing European regulatory consensus on youth energy drink access.
- Source: Healthline; Horus X (2025)
- Severity: Medium
- DB Status: New

#### Adolescent Health Risk — FDA and Pediatric Concerns
- Status: Pending (ongoing regulatory scrutiny)
- Date: 2020–2025
- Details: The FDA and American Academy of Pediatrics have consistently flagged energy drinks as inappropriate for adolescents. At 80mg caffeine per 8.4oz can, adolescents (ages 12–19) may experience heart palpitations, dehydration, sleep disruption, and behavioral changes. The recommended caffeine limit for adolescents is 100mg/day, meaning a single Red Bull accounts for 80% of that limit. Red Bull continued sponsoring youth-adjacent extreme sports events throughout this period with sponsorship payments exceeding €1 billion in 2022 alone.
- Source: EFSA, 2022; Harvard School of Public Health; Healthline (2024)
- Severity: High

#### EPA Clean Air Act Settlement — Anhydrous Ammonia Safety Violations
- Status: Adjudicated
- Date: 2023 (settlement announced June 2023)
- Details: The EPA settled with Anheuser-Busch LLC for $537,000 in penalties for violations of the Clean Air Act's chemical accident prevention requirements and the Emergency Planning and Community Right-to-Know Act. The EPA had inspected three AB InBev facilities (Merrimack, NH; Fort Collins, CO; Fairfield, CA) between 2016–2019, finding failures in Process Safety Management. A 2018 ammonia release at Fort Collins injured two employees and sent six local residents to the hospital. #### Mexicali Referendum — Voter Rejection on Water Rights (Detailed)
- Status: Adjudicated (project cancelled)
- Date: March 2020
- Details: In a March 21–22, 2020 referendum, 76.1% of Mexicali voters rejected Constellation's $1.4 billion brewery already under construction. The brewery was projected to consume 20 million cubic meters of water annually from a desert aquifer fed by the Colorado River — already over-exploited and causing water shortages in Tijuana, Tecate, Rosarito, and Ensenada. Mexico's National Human Rights Commission found in February 2020 that the project's approval violated the human right to water. President López Obrador called the vote despite a COVID-19 pandemic (turnout was less than 5%). Constellation accepted the result and abandoned the project. Activists from Mexicali Resiste continued calling for accountability into 2021.
- Source: Dialogue Earth; Wine Industry Advisor, 2021; Mexicali Resiste / PR Newswire, 2021 (https://dialogue.earth/en/water/35110-constellation-brands-mexicali-chose-water-over-beer/)

#### Alpine Water Marketing vs. US Domestic Sourcing
- Status: Public Record
- Date: 2022–2023
- Details: Liquid Death originally marketed its water as sourced from the Austrian Alps. By 2023, labeling on cans confirmed the water is now bottled at facilities in Bland, Virginia or Mackay, Idaho — both domestic US locations. The company has not been subject to formal regulatory action over this sourcing shift, but the geographic pivot from premium European Alpine water to municipal-adjacent US spring sources may constitute a material change in product positioning that was not proactively communicated to consumers.
