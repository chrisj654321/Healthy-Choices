## The Hain Celestial Group (id: hain-celestial)

### Currently in DB
- `heavy_metals_baby` (high): Congressional investigation found Earth's Best and Hain Celestial baby foods contained elevated arsenic, lead, cadmium, and mercury. — US House Subcommittee Report, 2021

### Research Findings

#### Heavy Metals in Baby Food — Consolidated MDL Litigation (ongoing)
- Status: Pending (active MDL)
- Date: 2021–2026
- Details: The February 2021 House Subcommittee report found Earth's Best oatmeal and rice cereals exceeded 100 ppb inorganic arsenic (FDA action level) in half of brown rice products tested, and Hain brands contained 641 ppb of lead — over 150% of the company's own internal upper limit. Hundreds of lawsuits were consolidated into MDL-3101 (In re Baby Food Product Liability Litigation); as of February 2026, 389 actions remain pending. The State of New Mexico separately filed a civil enforcement action against the company. The claims allege autism spectrum disorder links and failure to warn.
- Source: US House Subcommittee Report, Feb 2021; Law360 MDL-3101 docket; Miller & Zois, April 2026
- Severity: High
- DB Status: Confirms existing; adds MDL scale, 389 pending cases, NM state enforcement action

#### SEC Enforcement — Internal Controls Failures / Channel Stuffing (2018, ongoing civil)
- Status: Adjudicated (SEC 2018); Pending (securities class action)
- Date: 2014–2026
- Details: In 2018 the SEC charged Hain Celestial with internal controls failures stemming from end-of-quarter "channel stuffing" — offering undisclosed incentives to major distributors (UNFI, Walmart) to accept excess inventory, inflating revenue recognition from FY2014–2016. The SEC did not fine the company due to cooperation; Hain neither admitted nor denied findings. A related shareholder class action (Gimpel et al. v. Hain Celestial) was filed; the 2nd U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals reversed dismissal in 2025, reinstating the nine-year-old case and returning it to the district court. Allegations include intentional deception about financial results and accounting compliance.
- Source: SEC Press Release 2018-277; Reuters/Yahoo Finance, 2025; Law360 (2nd Circ. ruling)
- Severity: High
- DB Status: New

#### Restructuring and $531M Annual Loss (2024–2025)
- Status: Public Record
- Date: 2023–2025
- Details: Hain's "Hain Reimagined" transformation plan, launched September 2023, targeted $125M in restructuring costs across FY2024–2025. The company posted a $531 million loss in FY2025, divesting ParmCrisps (sold to Our Home, Sept 2024) and Thinsters. The restructuring includes plant closures, manufacturing consolidation, and significant workforce reductions. The company's own disclosure acknowledges its operating model was built for a much larger business, inflating costs and slowing decision-making.
- Source: Baking Business, 2025; Hain Celestial investor day materials, 2023; SEC 8-K filings 2024–2025
- Severity: Medium
- DB Status: New

#### False Advertising Class Action — "Organic Plus Vanilla Soy Milk" Flavoring Claims
- Status: Pending
- Date: 2020
- Details: A federal class action filed in 2020 alleged Hain Celestial's vanilla soy milk flavoring was not compliant with FDA regulations governing "characterizing flavor" disclosures, constituting false advertising. The company moved to dismiss, briefing was filed November 2020. The case was part of a wave of over 91 similar suits filed by the same plaintiffs' counsel against producers of vanilla-containing products.
- Source: National Law Review / Lexology, Nov 2020
- Severity: Low
- DB Status: New

## Amy's Kitchen (id: amy-kitchen)

### Currently in DB
- `union_busting` (high): Workers at Amy's Kitchen facilities filed NLRB complaints in 2022 alleging retaliation for organizing; workers described poor conditions inconsistent with the brand's values. — Guardian, 2022

### Research Findings

#### NLRB Unfair Labor Practice Charges and San Jose Plant Closure (2022)
- Status: Adjudicated (NLRB settlement December 2022)
- Date: 2022
- Details: On June 1, 2022, Unite Here Local 19 filed multiple ULP charges alleging mandatory anti-union meetings, surveillance, threats, interrogations, and termination of two employees for organizing. In July 2022, Amy's Kitchen abruptly closed its San Jose facility — eliminating 331 jobs — just as workers were actively organizing. Unite Here stated the closure was retaliation. An NLRB settlement reached in December 2022 required Amy's Kitchen to reinstate fired worker Hector Guardado with back pay, prohibit interrogation of workers about union support, prohibit retaliation against unionizing workers, and prohibit coercion. The company also agreed to post NLRB notices.
- Source: Prism Reports, Dec 2022; NLRB Case 32-CA-296891; In These Times, 2022; Labor Notes, June 2022
- Severity: High
- DB Status: Updates "union_busting" — adds NLRB settlement details, Guardado back pay, San Jose closure of 331 jobs

#### Cal/OSHA Violations — Multiple Facilities (2019–2022)
- Status: Adjudicated
- Date: 2019–2022
- Details: Over the decade leading to 2022, OSHA fined Amy's Kitchen more than $100,000 to settle federal health and safety violations at California locations. In 2019, Cal/OSHA opened three separate inspections at the Santa Rosa plant following accidents; regulators found 7 serious violations and issued $89,100 in penalties. A subsequent 2022 Cal/OSHA inspection — triggered by worker complaint in January 2022 — found 13 new citations including 3 "serious" violations (substandard emergency eyewash stations in boiler room, unsecured guards on dough-flattening conveyors), resulting in $25,000 in fines. The company also paid $23,135 and $6,000 to settle OSHA violations in Idaho and Oregon facilities in 2018.
- Source: KQED, 2022; American Prospect, 2022; NBC News, 2022
- Severity: High
- DB Status: New

#### Worker Injury and Ergonomic Hazards
- Status: Alleged / Documented
- Date: 2019–2022
- Details: Workers and investigative journalists documented that production line speeds required employees to roll 10–12 burritos per minute with inadequate task rotation, leading to repetitive motion injuries. Worker Cecilia Luna Ojeda sustained injuries requiring casts on both arms. Locked fire exits, denied restroom breaks, and a pattern of returning injured workers to the line without adequate recovery time were documented by multiple news investigations. Teamsters challenged Amy's Kitchen's B Corp certification in March 2022 due to documented worker abuses.
- Source: The Real News Network, 2022; American Prospect, 2022; Teamsters press release, March 2022
- Severity: High
- DB Status: New

## Bob's Red Mill Natural Foods (id: bobs-red-mill)

### Currently in DB
- (no existing issues)

### Research Findings

#### Cadmium in Flaxseed Products — Class Action Lawsuit and Prop 65 Notice (2022–2023)
- Status: Pending (class action); Alleged
- Date: 2022–2023
- Details: ConsumerLab testing (January 2023) found Bob's Red Mill Whole Ground Flaxseed Meal contained 6.1 mcg cadmium per serving, and Whole Golden Flaxseed Meal contained 5.5 mcg — both exceeding California Prop 65's warning trigger of 4.1 mcg. A California Prop 65 60-day notice was filed (2022-00760). A federal class action, Mark Hayden v. Bob's Red Mill Natural Foods Inc., Case No. 4:23-cv-03862 (N.D. Cal.), followed in 2023, alleging the products are advertised as healthy while containing unsafe cadmium levels not disclosed to consumers. Cadmium is a classified human carcinogen and reproductive toxin.
- Source: ConsumerLab, Jan & March 2023; ClassAction.org; TopClassActions.com; CA AG Prop 65 notice 2022-00760
- Severity: Medium
- DB Status: New

#### Salmonella Recall — Organic Amaranth Grain (2022)
- Status: Adjudicated (voluntary recall)
- Date: August 2022
- Details: Bob's Red Mill voluntarily recalled its Organic Amaranth Grain Gluten Free (22 oz.) due to potential Salmonella contamination. The recall covered distribution across 17 US states (CA, CT, FL, GA, IL, IN, KY, MD, NC, NH, NJ, NV, NY, OR, PA, TX, WA) and internationally including the Philippines. No illnesses were confirmed at time of recall.
- Source: FDA Recall Notice; Food Safety News, Aug 2022
- Severity: Medium
- DB Status: New

#### Glyphosate in Organic Oats — Class Action Lawsuit (2018, historical)
- Status: Adjudicated / Resolved
- Date: 2018
- Details: A federal class action was filed in San Francisco after glyphosate (Roundup) was detected in both Bob's Red Mill organic and non-organic oats. The lawsuit alleged the company misled consumers by advertising products as "pure and healthy" while containing a chemical linked to cancer. This was part of a broader industry-wide testing wave. Note: EWG's 2023 follow-up testing indicated overall glyphosate levels in oat products have declined across the industry.
- Source: Health Freedom Idaho; Organic Consumers Association; EWG, April 2023
- Severity: Medium
- DB Status: New (historical, pre-2020)

## Nature's Path Foods (id: natures-path)

### Currently in DB
- (no existing issues)

### Research Findings

#### Listeria Recall — Frozen Waffles (2024)
- Status: Adjudicated (recall completed, FDA terminated)
- Date: October 2024
- Details: Nature's Path Organic Foods recalled eleven frozen waffle varieties due to potential Listeria monocytogenes contamination. The recall resulted from routine sampling by TreeHouse Foods (Nature's Path's co-manufacturer for frozen waffles), which detected the bacteria. The recall was part of a broader TreeHouse Foods-initiated recall covering 40+ brands of frozen waffles and pancakes. No confirmed illnesses were reported. Listeria poses serious risks to pregnant women, the elderly, and immunocompromised individuals.
- Source: FDA Recall Notice (TreeHouse Foods expansion, Oct 22, 2024); Nature's Path press release; Consumer Reports, Oct 2024
- Severity: Medium
- DB Status: New

#### Peanut Allergen Recall — Organic Pumpkin Spice and Dark Chocolate Chip Waffles (2023)
- Status: Adjudicated (voluntary recall)
- Date: October 2023
- Details: Nature's Path voluntarily recalled specific batches of Organic Pumpkin Spice Waffles and Organic Dark Chocolate Chip Waffles due to undeclared peanuts, which could cause serious reactions in people with peanut allergies. The contamination resulted from a manufacturing error at an external co-manufacturer.
- Source: FDA Safety Alert; EatThis.com, Oct 2023; Nature's Path press release
- Severity: Medium
- DB Status: New

#### Protein Content Mislabeling — Class Action Lawsuit (2021–2022)
- Status: Pending (survived motion to dismiss)
- Date: 2021–2022
- Details: A federal class action alleged Nature's Path misled consumers about the protein content of Heritage Flakes, Flax Plus Raisin Bran, Flax Plus Multibran Flakes, and Heritage Original Crunch cereals. Plaintiff Ian Miller claimed the company failed to include correct protein daily value percentages on packaging in violation of FDA regulations and California's Sherman Law. A federal judge denied Nature's Path's motion to dismiss, allowing the case to proceed.
- Source: Courthouse News Service; TopClassActions.com
- Severity: Low
- DB Status: New

#### Gluten-Free Labeling Recall — Allergen Cross-Contamination (2019, historical)
- Status: Adjudicated (voluntary recall)
- Date: 2019
- Details: Nature's Path recalled over 450,000 boxes of gluten-free cereal after the products were found to potentially contain undeclared wheat and barley from a "production scheduling mixup" causing air contamination. This recall predates the 2020 research window but is relevant context for recurring allergen control weaknesses.
- Source: Legal Reader; Nature's Path Foods Food Safety News tag
- Severity: Medium

## KIND Snacks (id: kind-snacks)

### Currently in DB
- `fda_healthy` (low): FDA initially sent KIND a warning letter (2015) claiming bars didn't meet the "healthy" definition due to fat from nuts; FDA later revised its stance. — FDA, 2015–2022

### Research Findings

#### Mars Full Acquisition Completed (2020)
- Status: Public Record
- Date: November 2020
- Details: Mars Inc. completed full acquisition of KIND North America in November 2020 (it had held a minority stake since 2017). KIND is now a wholly owned Mars subsidiary, bringing it under a parent company with documented issues including child labor in West African cocoa supply chains and a primarily confectionery-focused portfolio. The acquisition valued KIND at approximately $5 billion.
- Source: BusinessWire / NOSH, November 2020; Food Dive
- Severity: Medium
- DB Status: New — updates "fda_healthy" context; Mars ownership is material for transparency

#### FDA "Healthy" Label Resolution (2022)
- Status: Adjudicated (regulatory)
- Date: 2015–2022
- Details: After FDA's 2015 warning letter, KIND filed a citizen petition challenging the FDA's "healthy" definition, arguing nuts' unsaturated fats should qualify. FDA exercised enforcement discretion and in 2022 finalized a new "healthy" nutrient content claim definition that permits foods with nuts and seeds to use the term, effectively vindicating KIND's position. The case helped reshape FDA labeling policy across the industry.
- Source: FDA, 2022; KIND Wikipedia; FDA Healthy Claim update
- Severity: Low
- DB Status: Updates "fda_healthy" — case now resolved in KIND's favor; DB entry's framing is slightly outdated

#### Mars Parent Company ESG Concerns
- Status: Public Record
- Date: 2020–2025
- Details: As a Mars subsidiary, KIND is now part of a conglomerate with documented high-severity issues: (1) child labor in Ivory Coast and Ghana cocoa supply chains (Washington Post, 2019; ongoing); (2) $0.9B KIND revenue folded into Mars' $47B revenue base dominated by confectionery. Consumers who choose KIND for its "health snack" positioning may not be aware they are purchasing from the world's largest candy company with unresolved cocoa supply chain ethics issues.
- Source: Mars DB entry; Washington Post, 2019; Forbes, 2022
- Severity: Medium
- DB Status: New

## Simply Good Foods Company (id: simply-good-foods)

### Currently in DB
- `sweetener_load` (medium): Quest and Atkins products rely heavily on sucralose, erythritol, and other sugar alcohols; emerging research raises questions about long-term metabolic effects. — Nature Medicine, 2023

### Research Findings

#### Erythritol Cardiovascular Risk Study (2023)
- Status: Alleged (scientific publication, not regulatory action)
- Date: 2023
- Details: A major study published in Nature Medicine (March 2023) found that high blood levels of erythritol were associated with increased risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (heart attack, stroke) in a large cohort of at-risk patients. The study also showed that consuming erythritol caused immediate elevation of blood erythritol levels. Quest and Atkins products use erythritol extensively as a primary sweetener. The FDA has not taken regulatory action; the industry disputes the study's applicability to dietary intake levels. However, the research directly implicates product categories central to Simply Good Foods' portfolio.
- Source: Nature Medicine, March 2023 (Hazen et al., Cleveland Clinic); CSPI response, 2023
- Severity: Medium
- DB Status: Updates "sweetener_load" — adds specific erythritol/cardiovascular risk citation and publication details

#### Atkins Frozen Meal Listeria Recall (2024)
- Status: Adjudicated (voluntary recall)
- Date: October 2024
- Details: Specific lots of Atkins frozen meals were recalled due to potential Listeria monocytogenes contamination as part of a broader recall from co-manufacturer. Affected products included Atkins Chicken Margherita (9 oz.) and Atkins Chicken & Broccoli Alfredo (9 oz.), removed from Meijer shelves among other retailers.
- Source: SpartanNash Recall Notice, November 2024
- Severity: Medium
- DB Status: New

#### Quest Nutrition Prop 65 Notice — Cookies and Cream Protein Bars (2022)

## Beyond Meat, Inc. (id: beyond-meat)

### Currently in DB
- `processing_beyond` (medium): Despite plant-based positioning, Beyond Meat products are ultra-processed with methylcellulose, modified starch, and high sodium. — NOVA food classification, 2023

### Research Findings

#### $7.5 Million False Advertising Settlement — Protein Content Overstatement (2025)
- Status: Adjudicated (settlement approved)
- Date: 2018–2024 purchase window; settled 2025
- Details: Beyond Meat settled a $7.5 million class action over allegations that it knowingly overstated the protein content and quality of its products by up to 30%. The lawsuit alleged Beyond Meat used incorrect testing methods for calculating protein content in violation of FDA regulations, making protein claims on packaging misleading. Settlement covered purchases of Beyond Burgers, sausages, tenders, and meatballs between May 2018 and August 2024. Class members received $2 per product (max $10 without proof of purchase). Claim deadline was April 14, 2025.
- Source: TopClassActions.com; BeyondMeatProteinSettlement.com; OpenClassActions.com
- Severity: Medium
- DB Status: New

#### Securities Fraud Class Action — Scale and Production Misrepresentations (2023)
- Status: Pending
- Date: 2020–2022 class period; filed 2023
- Details: A securities class action alleges Beyond Meat executives made materially false and misleading statements about the company's ability to produce plant-based meats at scale during the class period of May 5, 2020 to October 13, 2022. The case involves investor losses tied to alleged misrepresentations about production capacity and growth trajectory.
- Source: Bleichmar Fonti & Auld investigation; GlobeNewswire, 2023
- Severity: High
- DB Status: New

#### Securities Fraud Class Action — Asset Impairment Misrepresentations (2025–2026)
- Status: Pending
- Date: 2025–2026
- Details: A second securities class action, filed in early 2026 (class period February 27, 2025 – November 11, 2025), alleges Beyond Meat knew that certain long-lived assets were worth far less than book value but delayed disclosing this. Investors suffered losses when the impairments were ultimately recognized. Levi & Korsinsky is pursuing claims on behalf of BYND shareholders.
- Source: GlobeNewswire / Levi & Korsinsky, March 2026; ClassyAction.app, 2026
- Severity: High
- DB Status: New

#### Food Safety Issues — Listeria, Mold, and Salmonella at Pennsylvania Facility (2022)
- Status: Alleged (internal documents; company disputes)
- Date: 2021–2022
- Details: Internal Beyond Meat spreadsheets leaked to media showed 11 confirmed Listeria incidents in the Pennsylvania facility between June 2021–June 2022, 11 instances of salmonella out of 56 tests, and incidents of mold. Additionally, wood was found by consumers in burgers at A&W restaurants (October 2021) and a metal link was found in Dunkin' sausage (April 2021). The FDA had not inspected the facility since Beyond Meat purchased it. Beyond Meat disputed findings, noting Pennsylvania Dept. of Agriculture found no non-conformances in March and September 2022 inspections.
- Source: Spokesman Review (leaked documents), Nov 2022; AboutLawsuits.com; Food Processing, 2022

- DB Status: New

Charged with terroristic threatening and third-degree battery. Ramsey was suspended September 20, 2022 and left the company October 14, 2022. He pleaded guilty to felony criminal mischief and misdemeanor disorderly conduct in February 2023, receiving three years' probation and a $1,000 fine.

## Impossible Foods (id: impossible-foods)

### Currently in DB
- `gmo_soy` (medium): Impossible Burger's soy leghemoglobin is produced via genetically modified yeast; long-term safety data is limited. — FDA GRAS Notice, 2019
- `sodium_impossible` (medium): Impossible Burger contains 370mg sodium per 4oz patty. — CSPI, 2022

### Research Findings

#### Center for Food Safety Lawsuit Over Soy Leghemoglobin (GMO Heme) — 9th Circuit Ruling (2021)
- Status: Adjudicated (FDA approval upheld)
- Date: 2020–2021
- Details: The Center for Food Safety filed suit in March 2020 challenging the FDA's 2019 approval of soy leghemoglobin as a color additive, arguing FDA did not apply the correct "convincing evidence of no harm" standard. The 9th Circuit ruled in May 2021 that FDA applied the correct standard and upheld the approval, even though preliminary animal lab data submitted by Impossible Foods indicated potential health risks including blood clot issues, anemia, and kidney damage. The court held that concerned consumers had standing to challenge FDA determinations but found the approval legally sound. Impossible Foods' heme can continue to be used.
- Source: Center for Food Safety press release; Food Dive, May 2021; FindLaw (9th Cir. 2021)
- Severity: Medium
- DB Status: Updates "gmo_soy" — adds court outcome, animal lab data concerns (anemia, blood clots, kidney damage), and final legal resolution

#### Metal Foreign Object Recall — Ground Sausage (2024)
- Status: Adjudicated (voluntary recall)
- Date: May–November 2024
- Details: Impossible Foods initiated a voluntary recall of ground sausage products due to potential presence of aluminum foreign material. Aluminum packaging fasteners from a co-manufacturer (OSI Industries) were found inside raw sausage products. Affected products were distributed between May 14, 2024 and November 5, 2024. The recall was classified by FDA with a risk level assessment.
- Source: Newsweek, Dec 2024; FSIS recall records; SpartanNash recall notice
- Severity: Medium
- DB Status: New

#### Significant Workforce Reductions / Financial Distress (2022–2024)
- Status: Public Record
- Date: 2022–2024
- Details: Impossible Foods cut 132 workers in a California WARN Act filing in 2022, followed by additional layoffs of 140 employees in 2023 and further buyouts. Supermarket plant-based meat purchases were down approximately 14% (IRI data) as consumer demand declined. The company has not yet achieved profitability and has raised over $2 billion from investors including Bill Gates, Google Ventures, and Khosla Ventures without reaching breakeven.
- Source: Food Dive, 2022; LinkedIn/SunsetHQ layoff tracker, 2023–2024
- Severity: Low
- DB Status: New

## Oatly Group AB (id: oatly)

### Currently in DB
- `rapeseed_oil` (medium): Oatly uses canola/rapeseed oil in its barista blends; despite oat-focused marketing, oil content raises processing concerns. — Vox, 2021
- `blackstone_investment` (medium): 2020 Blackstone investment — fund associated with Amazon deforestation financing — drew significant backlash. — Guardian, 2020

### Research Findings

#### $9.25 Million Greenwashing / Securities Fraud Settlement (2024)
- Status: Adjudicated (settlement approved 2024)
- Date: 2021–2024
- Details: Following a July 2021 short seller report by Spruce Point Capital Management, investor class actions alleged Oatly: (a) overinflated gross margins, revenue, capital expenditure, and market share metrics; (b) overstated sustainability practices and environmental impact; (c) exaggerated growth in China; and (d) had run through three auditors in six years without disclosure. Oatly agreed to a $9.25 million settlement (approved 2024) to resolve the securities litigation. The case is In re Oatly Group AB Securities Litigation (climate litigation database). Oatly's stock declined 7.8% in two trading sessions after the Spruce Point report published July 14, 2021.
- Source: Bloomberg Law; Vegconomist; PracticalESG; The Plant Base, 2024; Climate Litigation Database
- Severity: High
- DB Status: New

#### UK Advertising Standards Authority Ban — Misleading Environmental Claims (2022)
- Status: Adjudicated (ASA ruling)
- Date: January 2022
- Details: The UK Advertising Standards Authority banned multiple Oatly TV, print, and social media advertisements after receiving 109 complaints. The core disputed claim — "Oatly generates 73% less CO2e vs. milk" — was found misleading because it was only valid for one product (Barista Edition) compared to whole cow's milk, but was presented as applying to all Oatly products and all milk types. The ASA also found Oatly's claim that dairy and meat industries emit more CO2e "than all the world's planes, trains, cars, boats etc. combined" overstated emissions. Oatly was directed to ensure the basis and scope of any environmental claim is made clear.
- Source: ASA Ruling (Oatly UK Ltd, G21-1096286); ITV News, Jan 26 2022; FoodNavigator, Jan 2022; Euronews, Jan 2022
- Severity: Medium
- DB Status: New

#### Blackstone Investment — Deforestation and Political Links (2020)
- Status: Public Record
- Date: July 2020
- Details: Oatly accepted $200 million from an investment group led by Blackstone in July 2020 (~10% stake, valuing Oatly at ~£2 billion). Backlash was immediate: Blackstone holds ownership stakes in Hidrovias do Brasil and Pátria Investimento, Brazilian firms linked to Amazon deforestation. Blackstone CEO Stephen Schwarzman donated $3 million to Donald Trump's 2020 reelection campaign. High-profile co-investors included Oprah Winfrey, Jay-Z, and Natalie Portman. Consumer boycott campaigns launched on social media; Oatly defended the investment as necessary to "speak a language capital markets understand."
- Source: Guardian, 2020; CNBC; Food Dive; Vice; FoodNavigator, Sept 2020
- Severity: Medium
- DB Status: Confirms "blackstone_investment" — adds deforestation entity names (Hidrovias do Brasil, Pátria Investimento), Schwarzman's $3M Trump donation, and investment amount ($200M)

#### Auditor Turnover and Accounting Anomalies (2021)
- Status: Alleged (short seller report; no SEC enforcement found)
- Date: 2021
- Details: Spruce Point Capital Management's July 2021 report identified that Oatly had cycled through three auditors in six years, which it described as "highly unusual" and not disclosed in IPO filings. The report also alleged anomalies in sales recognition, gross margin reporting, inventory accounting, and capital expenditure. These allegations formed the basis of the securities litigation that ultimately settled for $9.25M.
- Source: Spruce Point Capital report, July 14 2021; CNBC, July 2021; FoodNavigator USA, July 2021
- Severity: Medium
- DB Status: New (supports and elaborates on the securities settlement above)
