## Garden of Life (garden-of-life)

**FTC deceptive advertising ($2.2M, 2021, protein powder)** — ❌ REMOVE as written / 📅 OUTDATED actual event.
The research's specifics are fabricated. The real FTC action was in **2006**: Garden of Life and founder Jordan Rubin paid **$225,000** in consumer redress to settle charges of unsubstantiated disease-treatment claims for Primal Defense, RM-10, Living Multi and FYI — NOT a $2.2M 2021 protein-powder settlement. 20 years old; no ongoing relevance. Will not state the fake version. May reference founder/FTC history only in hedged, dated terms, but cleaner to omit.
Source: FTC Press Release, March 2006.

**Lead in protein powder (FDA warning letter / recall, 2023)** — ⚠️ ALLEGED (reframed).
There was **no FDA warning letter or recall**. The real basis: **Consumer Reports 2025** found Garden of Life Sport Organic Plant-Based Protein at ~61 ppb lead (well above CR's own 0.5 µg/day guidance). FDA has set no enforceable lead limit for protein powders. A consumer **class-action lawsuit** (pending) alleges the product was falsely advertised as safe. Use hedged language: testing finding + pending suit. Severity MEDIUM, not HIGH (no recall, no injuries, no adjudication).
Source: Consumer Reports, 2025; Top Class Actions (pending litigation).

**Probiotic potency below label (ConsumerLab, 2024)** — ⚠️ ALLEGED. ConsumerLab independent testing is credible media-grade but not regulatory adjudication. Hedge. Severity LOW–MEDIUM.

**Nestlé ownership / cocoa supply-chain labor** — ⚠️ ALLEGED, attenuated. Garden of Life is Nestlé-owned (verified). Linking GoL products to Côte d'Ivoire cocoa labor is speculative for this brand (GoL is supplements, not chocolate). Keep only as ownership-conflict disclosure, hedged. Severity LOW.

## Lundberg (lundberg)

**Arsenic in rice** — ✅ VERIFIED (endemic, hedged). Consumer Reports' rice-arsenic testing is real; independent testing (Tamara Rubin, Oct 2024) confirms detectable inorganic arsenic/cadmium in California rice. BUT Lundberg's own published testing averages ~90 ppb, below FDA/EU thresholds, and the issue is endemic to all California/US rice, not Lundberg-specific misconduct. Present as category/health-context disclosure. Severity MEDIUM (not HIGH — no enforcement, below limits).
Source: Consumer Reports (rice arsenic); Tamara Rubin lab report 2024.

**Lead in rice bran/polish (NSF, 2024, exceeds Prop 65)** — ⚠️ ALLEGED. Could not independently confirm an NSF-published Lundberg-specific lead finding; trace heavy metals in rice byproducts are plausible/endemic but the specific "exceeds Prop 65 child reference dose" claim is unverified. Hedge heavily or fold into the arsenic/heavy-metals disclosure. Severity MEDIUM at most.

**Water/irrigation (Sacramento Valley drought)** — ⚠️ ALLEGED/contextual. California drought + ag water allocation is real public context; "community opposition to Lundberg allocations" specifically is unverified. Keep as general sustainability context, hedged. Severity LOW–MEDIUM.

**Labor / no OSHA-NLRB violations** — ✅ VERIFIED (negative). Positive note. Family-owned, Fair Trade certs on some organic rice. Severity LOW.

## Eden Foods (eden-foods)

The research claim is the opposite of reality and defamatory. Remove entirely. If anything Eden's BPA-free leadership is a positive.
Source: edenfoods.com/BPA; Packaging Digest.

**Supply-chain transparency (small private co.)** — ⚠️ ALLEGED/soft. Generic observation about limited disclosure; acceptable only as mild, hedged context. Severity LOW.

Severity LOW–MEDIUM.
