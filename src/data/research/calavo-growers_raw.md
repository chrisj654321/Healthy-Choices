## Issues

- **FDA Warning Letter — October 25, 2024** (CMS # 687785), addressed to Chantal Vos, Director of Hawaii Operations, Calavo Growers, Inc. Followed an FDA domestic investigation of the company's papaya packing/holding operation on April 16, 2024, and inspection of a contracted growing farm April 20–22, 2024. Findings: violation of the Produce Safety regulation (21 CFR Part 112) — the contracted farm did not provide adequate, readily accessible toilet facilities for workers in growing areas during harvesting; workers reportedly had to use nearby bushes because the sole toilet was located 0.30 miles from the harvest site. **This was a repeat finding** — the same type of violation was observed in a prior 2023 inspection at the same/similar site. Calavo representatives acknowledged the violations and verbally committed to relocating toilet facilities and adding composting toilets across contracted farms. This is an adjudicated FDA Warning Letter (formal compliance action), not merely alleged. Source: FDA, "Calavo Growers Inc 687785 10/25/2024," fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/calavo-growers-inc-687785-10252024 (accessed 2026-08-22); also reported by Food Safety News, "FDA cites Calavo Growers for repeat toilet facility violations," foodsafetynews.com/2024/12/fda-cites-calavo-growers-for-repeat-toilet-facility-violations-mena-food-group-for-food-safety-and-labeling-failures/ (Dec 2024).

- **FCPA investigation — Mexico operations (ongoing/closed, no enforcement action):**
  - January 16, 2024: Calavo announced that an internal audit process had identified matters warranting enhanced evaluation by the Audit Committee/Board; a Special Committee (with external legal counsel and forensic accountants) was formed.
  - The Special Committee determined that certain matters related to Calavo's Mexico operations raised potential issues under the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA).
  - Calavo voluntarily disclosed the internal investigation to both the SEC and DOJ and stated it would cooperate fully.
  - January 31, 2024 (after market hours): Calavo issued a press release announcing partial results of the Special Committee investigation; this also came alongside a delayed FY2023 10-K filing (Form 12b-25 Notification of Late Filing).
  - February 10 and February 18, 2025: Executive Orders under the Trump administration paused/deprioritized FCPA enforcement broadly (EO pausing enforcement for 180 days; second EO on agency accountability). Around this time SEC notified Calavo the investigation activity had been "postponed" (disclosed in Calavo's quarterly filing dated March 13, 2025).
  - **September 2, 2025: DOJ formally notified Calavo it had closed its FCPA inquiry into the Mexico operations, without enforcement action.** Source: SEC 8-K exhibit, sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1133470/000155837025012019/cvgw-20250909xex99d1.htm (filed 2025-09-09; direct fetch blocked HTTP 403, confirmed via search-index summary).
  - **December 22, 2025: SEC staff sent Calavo a letter stating it "has concluded its investigation relating to the company and, based on the information available as of that date, does not intend to recommend an enforcement action."**
  - **No fine, penalty, consent order, or admission of wrongdoing resulted from either agency's investigation — both closed without enforcement action.**
  - Source (timeline aggregation): FCPA Professor, "Calavo Growers – 'Activity In The [SEC] Investigation Has Been Postponed,'" fcpaprofessor.com/calavo-growers-activity-in-the-sec-investigation-has-been-postponed/ (accessed 2026-08-22); Compliance Week, "Calavo Growers discloses potential FCPA issues," complianceweek.com/regulatory-enforcement/calavo-growers-discloses-potential-fcpa-issues/34259.article (search-index summary, accessed 2026-08-22).

- **Securities class actions — 2015, dismissed:**
  - Two related securities-fraud class actions filed in U.S. District Court, Central District of California, January 2015: *El Dabe v. Calavo Growers, Inc., et al.*, No. 2:15-cv-00400 (filed Jan 20, 2015), and *O'Hanlon v. Calavo Growers, Inc., et al.*, No. 2:15-cv-00414 (filed Jan 21, 2015).
  - Triggered by Calavo's January 15, 2015 announcement of a non-cash misstatement in historical consolidated financial statements related to accounting treatment of contingent consideration from the 2011 Renaissance Food Group acquisition; company recorded a non-cash charge of $88.9 million pre-tax ($54 million net of tax) via Form 8-K.
  - Complaint alleged violations of federal securities laws (materially misleading statements).
  - **Outcome: dismissed.** The district court dismissed the complaint twice — first with leave to amend, then with prejudice in February 2016. The Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals unanimously affirmed the dismissal in a 2018 ruling (Sherif El Dabe v. Calavo Growers, Inc., No. 16-55426, 9th Cir., decided Jan 10, 2018). **No settlement, no monetary recovery, no finding of liability.**
  - Source: CourtListener docket, courtlistener.com/docket/4152454/sherif-e-el-dabe-v-calavo-growers-inc/; Justia, law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/16-55426/16-55426-2018-01-10.html (accessed 2026-08-22).
