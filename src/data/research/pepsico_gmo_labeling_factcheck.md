## 4. Oregon Measure 92 (2014)

**"Defeated by 802 votes after recount — 50.03% No (753,574) to 49.97% Yes (752,737)"** — **DISPUTED**. The Ballotpedia page (directly fetched) contains **two** results tables: a "Recount" (final/certified) table showing No 753,574 / Yes 752,737 — a margin of **837 votes**, not 802 — and a separate "Initial count" table showing No 753,489 / Yes 752,687, a margin of exactly **802 votes**. The page's own narrative text says the measure "was initially defeated by a mere 802 votes" (i.e., 802 is the *initial-count* margin, pre-recount) and that the recount did not change the outcome. The raw file spliced the recount's vote totals (753,574/752,737) onto the initial count's 802-vote margin — the two numbers are from different tables and don't pair together. Correct pairing: initial count = 753,489 to 752,687 (802-vote margin); recount/certified = 753,574 to 752,737 (837-vote margin). Percentages (50.03%/49.97%) round the same either way, so those are fine.

**"Most expensive ballot measure in Oregon history... over $30 million combined"** — **VERIFIED**. Confirmed verbatim via direct fetch: "Measure 92 also became the most expensive ballot measure in the state's history, as of 2014, bringing in over $30 million."

**PepsiCo $2,350,000; Monsanto $5,958,750; DuPont Pioneer $4,518,150; Coca-Cola $1,170,000; DOW AgroSciences $1,157,150; all listed as out-of-state contributors** — **VERIFIED**. All figures confirmed exact in the top-5 contributors table, same page.

**Total opposition raised $20,881,101.73, spent $20,477,544.17, new fundraising record** — **VERIFIED**. Confirmed exact, same page ("set a new record for the most money ever raised in the state by one side of a ballot measure campaign").

**Michael Gruber, GMA VP Federal Affairs, listed on Citizens Panel** — **VERIFIED**. Confirmed via direct fetch, same page.

## 5. Federal endgame: National Bioengineered Food Disclosure Standard (2016)

**Public Law 114-216, "DARK Act" critique, preempted state GMO labeling laws (incl. Vermont's), signed by Obama July 29, 2016** — **VERIFIED**. Confirmed via Wikipedia fetch and cross-check; signing date matches exactly.

**"It passed the House 230-45 (July 2015)"** — **DISPUTED / likely incorrect**. Direct verification via Congress.gov roll-call data (H.R. 1599, Safe and Accurate Food Labeling Act, July 23, 2015, Roll Call #462) shows the actual House vote was **275–150**. "45" appears to be a garbled reference to the fact that 45 Democrats crossed over to vote yes (a sub-count, not the full tally); "230" doesn't match any reported figure for this vote. Additionally, H.R. 1599 itself never passed the Senate and never became law — the bill that actually became Public Law 114-216 was **S.764**, which passed the House 306–117 on July 14, 2016 (Senate 63–30 on June 23, 2016) before Obama signed it July 29, 2016. The raw file's "230-45 / July 2015" figure matches neither the H.R. 1599 vote nor the actual enacting S.764 vote. This should be corrected to either drop the vote count or cite the verified S.764 tally (306-117 House, 63-30 Senate) since that's the bill that actually became law.

**GMA $10.5M 2015 lobbying spend; $2.8M GMA-only anti-labeling spend since Jan. 2014 (34 lobbyists)** — **VERIFIED**. Confirmed exact via direct fetch of the EWG primary research page (both figures appear verbatim).

**Six companies (Coca-Cola, PepsiCo, Kellogg's, Kraft Heinz, Land O'Lakes, General Mills) — $20.6M in 2015, $47.9M since 2013** — **VERIFIED**. Confirmed exact, same EWG primary page.

**Industry-wide $101.4M in 2015; $192.8M cumulative since 2013** — **VERIFIED**. Confirmed exact, same EWG primary page.

**EWG's separate ~$200M ballot-opposition estimate, ~$400M combined (excludes state-legislature lobbying)** — **VERIFIED**. The "$200 million" figure is present on the same EWG primary page; combined framing is the raw file's own arithmetic/characterization of two verified EWG figures, not itself a separate sourced claim.

**PepsiCo-isolated federal lobbying figure for the 2016 bill: NOT FOUND** — **VERIFIED as an accurate "not found"**. No isolated PepsiCo-only figure for this specific bill surfaced in this fact-check pass either; the researcher's NOT-FOUND flag is itself correct.

**GMA SVP Karin Moore quote on the Dec. 2018 final rule** — **VERIFIED**. Direct fetch of progressivegrocer.com returned HTTP 403, but a targeted search surfaced the article's indexed text with the exact quote ("Consumers demand transparency, period...") matching the raw file verbatim. Flagged VERIFIED on the strength of the matching indexed primary-article text, though a live direct read was blocked.

**PepsiCo GMA membership through 2018 — Vivek Sankaran named to board-level CEO search committee** — **VERIFIED, and understated**. Direct fetch of a GMA/Consumer Brands Association press release (dated June 25, 2018 — primary source) shows Sankaran was elected **Chairman of the GMA Board of Directors** in 2018, not merely a search-committee member — stronger, more specific confirmation of PepsiCo's board-level GMA presence in 2018 than the raw file claimed. The underlying point (PepsiCo was GMA-affiliated at board level through 2018) is VERIFIED; the raw file's specific "search committee" framing is an understatement of what the primary source actually shows.

H.R. 1599 passed the House 275-150 on July 23, 2015 (Roll Call #462); the bill that actually became law, S.764, passed the House 306-117 on July 14, 2016. "45" appears to be a misapplied sub-count (Democrats voting yes on H.R. 1599), and "230" doesn't match any reported tally.
