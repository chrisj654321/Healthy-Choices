# R.M. Palmer Co. — Raw Research (Political/Legal Analysis Agent 1)

Company: R.M. Palmer Company, West Reading, PA (chocolate/candy manufacturer). Privately held.

All facts below are cited. ADJUDICATED = confirmed by a government finding/action. ALLEGED = claim in a filed civil complaint, not yet proven or admitted.

## Issues

### March 24, 2023 factory explosion — confirmed facts (ADJUDICATED / government-sourced)

- Date/location: A natural gas explosion occurred at R.M. Palmer Co.'s West Reading, PA facility (77 South 2nd Ave.) on March 24, 2023.
- Death toll: 7 workers killed. Source: [NTSB final report coverage, Philadelphia Inquirer, April 8 2025](https://www.inquirer.com/news/pennsylvania/ntsb-report-west-reading-fatal-explosion-20250408.html); [Wikipedia summary of NTSB/AP reporting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Pennsylvania_chocolate_factory_explosion).
- Injuries: 10 people injured; 3 neighboring families displaced from a damaged apartment building.
- Property damage: approx. $42 million.
- Cause (NTSB, final report issued ~April 2025): "probable cause" was degradation of a retired UGI Utilities natural-gas service tee (plastic pipe fitting) that allowed gas to leak and migrate underground into the R.M. Palmer buildings. NTSB also found R.M. Palmer's emergency-response procedures and employee training were insufficient, and employees who smelled gas did not evacuate before the blast. Source: [NTSB report coverage, WFMZ, April 8 2025](https://www.wfmz.com/news/area/berks/ntsb-issues-final-report-on-2023-r-m-palmer-chocolate-factory-explosion-that-killed-seven/article_af92c840-061a-417b-abc7-4d34592581ef.html).

### OSHA investigation and citations (ADJUDICATED — government enforcement action)

- OSHA opened a fatality/catastrophe inspection the same day as the explosion (inspection number 1659063.015, per OSHA's public establishment search).
- OSHA findings announced Oct. 5, 2023: R.M. Palmer did not evacuate the plant despite employees reporting they smelled natural gas before the explosion. OSHA cited the company under the General Duty Clause for failing to evacuate, plus additional citations for: emergency exit signage not clearly marked (a basement exit door was unmarked, nearest marked exit >100 ft away), improper flexible-cord/heat-tape splicing on chocolate pipes between buildings, and recordkeeping violations. Source: [U.S. Dept. of Labor/OSHA news release, Oct. 5 2023](https://www.dol.gov/newsroom/releases/osha/osha20231005); [WESA/NPR, Oct. 6 2023](https://www.wesa.fm/economy-business/2023-10-06/osha-chocolate-factory-explosion-findings); [Philadelphia Inquirer, Oct. 5 2023](https://www.inquirer.com/news/pennsylvania/west-reading-chocolate-factory-explosion-osha-report-evacuation-20231005.html).
- Citation count: 4 citations reported by news coverage of the Oct. 5, 2023 announcement (OSHA's public inspection-detail page, queried directly, shows a different/higher citation count — see Data-quality note below).
- Total proposed penalty: **$44,483** — reported consistently by CBS News Philadelphia and other outlets citing the Oct. 5, 2023 OSHA announcement ([CBS News Philadelphia](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/philadelphia/news/west-reading-explosion-rm-palmer-candy-factory)). One outlet (Food Processing magazine) reported the total as $45,500 ([Food Processing, Oct. 2023](https://www.foodprocessing.com/workforce/worker-safety/news/33012778/osha-fines-rm-palmer-for-not-evacuating-employees-in-fatal-chocolate-factory-explosion)) — a minor discrepancy between outlets, both citing the same Oct. 5, 2023 OSHA action.
- Company response: R.M. Palmer stated at the time (Oct. 2023) that it "intends to vigorously contest OSHA's citations, which it believes are legally and factually unsupported," and issued at least two public statements to this effect via PR Newswire and Berks Weekly. Sources: [PR Newswire, RM Palmer statement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/rm-palmer-issues-additional-statement-on-osha-press-release-re-citations-301949911.html); [Berks Weekly](https://berksweekly.com/news/r-m-palmer-responds-to-osha-press-release-and-citations/).
- **Current status: UNCONFIRMED with high confidence.** No news source found reports a final resolution (upheld, reduced, settled, or withdrawn) of the contested citations. OSHA's own public inspection-detail page returned inconsistent figures across two separate automated queries (one showing $48,947 initial/$65,464 current across 10 violations; another showing a single serious citation at $8,929 initial/$15,000 current with a "Formal Settlement" final order dated 06/24/2024). These OSHA.gov page reads are NOT reliable — see Data-quality note. **Do not cite a specific final penalty amount or settlement date beyond the $44,483 figure reported by news outlets at the time of the Oct. 5, 2023 citation.** Recommend a follow-up direct pull from OSHA's enforcement database (https://www.osha.gov/ords/imis/establishment.inspection_detail?id=1659063.015) or OSHRC docket if a confirmed final status is needed.

**Data-quality note:** Two automated fetches of OSHA's own establishment/inspection detail page for inspection 1659063.015 returned mutually inconsistent structured data (different total penalties, different citation counts, different per-citation amounts) in the same research session. This is likely an artifact of the fetch/summarization tool misreading OSHA's dynamic IMIS tables, not a verified primary-source figure. Treat the OSHA.gov structured penalty breakdown as unverified; the $44,483 figure from contemporaneous news reporting (CBS News Philadelphia, citing the Oct. 5, 2023 DOL announcement) is the more reliable number for this write-up.

### Civil litigation (ALLEGED — none of the below is adjudicated; all are claims in filed complaints)

- First wrongful-death suit: filed by the estate of Judith "Judy" Lopez-Moran (55, Reading, PA), one of the 7 killed, via Saltz, Mongeluzzi & Bendesky P.C., filed in April 2023. Alleges workers smelled gas and alerted management, but the company did not act; alleges the explosion was "foreseeable, predictable, and preventable." Defendants include R.M. Palmer Company and its affiliates, and UGI Corporation/UGI Utilities (gas supplier). Source: [SMBB law firm release](https://www.smbb.com/news-article/family-of-worker-killed-in-r-m-palmer-candy-plant-explosion-files-first-wrongful-death-negligence-lawsuit-against-the-company-and-its-gas-suppliers/); [WFMZ, April 2023](https://www.wfmz.com/news/area/berks/first-wrongful-death-lawsuit-filed-in-deadly-r-m-palmer-explosion/article_269eb390-d875-11ed-95e4-83cb754b9f84.html); [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/palmer-pennsylvania-chocolate-factory-explosion-lawsuit/).
- Volume of litigation: By 2025, at least 27 lawsuits had been filed by victims, family members of the deceased, and neighbors against R.M. Palmer, UGI Utilities, and the manufacturer of the plastic gas line, per Philadelphia Inquirer reporting. Source: [Philadelphia Inquirer, July 29 2025](https://www.inquirer.com/news/philadelphia/rm-chocolate-factory-explosion-lawsuits-philadelphia-court-20250729.html).
- Venue: R.M. Palmer and UGI sought to move the cases to Berks County; a Philadelphia Common Pleas judge (Gwendolyn Bright) ruled in Sept. 2024/2025 that the cases stay in Philadelphia. Source: same Inquirer article above.
- Status as of latest available reporting (July 2025): cases remain pending in Philadelphia Court of Common Pleas. No reported settlement or trial verdict found in this research pass. These remain ALLEGATIONS against R.M. Palmer, not findings of liability.
