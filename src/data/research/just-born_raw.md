## Issues

### Consumer/product litigation — slack-fill class actions (candy box empty space)
- **White v. Just Born, Inc.**, Case No. 2:17-cv-04025-C-NKL, U.S. District Court, Western District of Missouri. Filed 2017 by plaintiff Daryl White Jr. Alleged Mike & Ike and Hot Tamales boxes contained approx. 35% non-functional slack-fill (empty space). A motion to dismiss was denied July 21, 2017, allowing the case to proceed. (Source: https://www.confectionerynews.com/Article/2017/08/01/Mike-Ike-Hot-Tamales-and-Jujyfruits-slack-fill-lawsuits-to-proceed/; https://dockets.justia.com/docket/missouri/mowdce/2:2017cv04025/132245)
- **Escobar et al. v. Just Born, Inc.** and **Mateski v. Just Born**, consolidated, Docket 17-cv-1826, U.S. District Court, Central District of California. Originally filed in state court February 2017, removed to federal court March 2017. Complaint alleged Mike & Ike and Hot Tamales boxes were "46% empty space." (Source: https://truthinadvertising.org/class-action/boxes-mike-ike-hot-tamales-candy/; https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/5940692/stephanie-escobar-v-just-born-inc/)
- **Settlement:** $3.3 million nationwide class settlement. Preliminary approval July 2020; final fairness hearing scheduled December 15, 2020. Settlement terms: class members with proof of purchase received $0.50 per product purchased or a voucher for one free box per two purchased (max 8 free boxes); those without proof of purchase received a voucher. Just Born also agreed to add either an image showing actual candy-piece size or a fill line to product labeling. (Source: https://truthinadvertising.org/class-action/boxes-mike-ike-hot-tamales-candy/; https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/you-could-be-eligible-to-receive-a-payment-from-a-class-action-settlement-if-you-purchased-a-box-of-candy-301112644.html)
- Status: **Settled** (civil class action, not a government/regulatory enforcement action). No FTC action identified in connection with these suits.

### Labor dispute — 2016 strike and related litigation
- Approximately 400 members of BCTGM (Bakery, Confectionery, Tobacco Workers and Grain Millers International Union) Local 6 at the Just Born Bethlehem, PA plant went on strike September 7, 2016, during peak Peeps production season, after rejecting the company's contract offer. Contract issues cited: proposed elimination of the pension plan for new hires, below-market wage increases, increased employee share of health insurance costs. Workers returned to work in late October 2016; a new contract had not been reached as of the report date. (Source: https://www.labornotes.org/2016/10/all-tricks-no-treat-peeps-candy-strike; https://portside.org/2016-09-26/who-will-win-just-born-game-chicken)
- **Just Born, Inc. v. Local Union No. 6, BCTGM**, Civil No. 16-5114, U.S. District Court, Eastern District of Pennsylvania. Just Born sued the union alleging the September 2016 strike violated a no-strike clause in the expired collective bargaining agreement. U.S. District Judge Jeffrey L. Schmehl granted summary judgment for the union and dismissed the case December 29, 2017 (2017 WL 6731647), ruling Just Born failed to show the workers had waived their right to strike. (Source: https://aflcio.org/2018/1/4/federal-judge-dismisses-just-born-lawsuit-against-bctgm-local-6; https://www.bredhoff.com/news/december-29-2017; https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/USCOURTS-paed-5_16-cv-05114/context)
- Status: **Adjudicated** — company's suit against the union dismissed via summary judgment.
- No specific NLRB unfair-labor-practice case number against Just Born was independently located/confirmed in this search (general references to an NLRB bargaining matter appeared in secondary sources but a case number could not be verified).

### Ingredient/advocacy pressure — Red Dye No. 3
- March 2023: Consumer Reports and allied consumer-advocacy organizations sent a letter to Just Born Quality Confections urging removal of Red Dye No. 3 from its candies (including Peeps), citing its status as a carcinogen banned by the FDA in cosmetics since 1990. This followed a late-2022 petition by 20+ organizations asking the FDA to prohibit Red Dye No. 3 in food, dietary supplements, and ingested drugs. (Source: https://advocacy.consumerreports.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Just-Born-Confectioners-Letter.pdf — PDF; exact date within the document not machine-extractable from this fetch, filename/upload dated April 2023 on the hosting server)
- This is an **advocacy-group letter, not an FDA warning letter or other regulatory enforcement action.**
