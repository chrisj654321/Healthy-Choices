## PRIORITY — Pork antitrust: defendant or purchaser? (role-confusion cross-check)

**Claim as raised in raw file:** Hormel Foods, LLC is a named party in *In re
Pork Antitrust Litigation* (0:18-cv-01776 / MDL 2998, D. Minnesota), alongside
Agri Stats, Seaboard Foods, Seaboard Corp, Smithfield Foods. Raw file marks
this VERIFIED off the Stage 1 CourtListener pull.

**Resolution: UPHELD. Hormel's role is DEFENDANT (alleged price-fixer), not
purchaser/plaintiff** — this is the opposite fact pattern from the Kraft
Heinz broiler-chicken case in the parallel fact-check, and independently
confirmed here, not assumed from plausibility.

Independent WebSearch (LegalClarity case summary, cross-checked against
Hunton Andrews Kurth/Lockridge Grindal Nauen-style case trackers and
meatingplace.com/nationalhogfarmer.com trade coverage) consistently lists the
**defendant roster** as: Agri Stats, Inc.; Clemens Food Group, LLC; The
Clemens Family Corporation; Hormel Foods Corporation; Hormel Foods, LLC; JBS
USA Food Company; Seaboard Foods LLC; Smithfield Foods, Inc.; Triumph Foods,
LLC; Tyson Foods, Inc.; Tyson Prepared Foods, Inc.; Tyson Fresh Meats, Inc.
Hormel is squarely on the *defendant* side across every independent source
found — no source anywhere places Hormel on the purchaser/plaintiff side.
This is consistent with the underlying allegation (pork producers conspiring
to restrict hog supply, 2009–2018+) which by its nature names producers as
defendants and purchasers (restaurants, wholesalers, consumers) as
plaintiffs — Hormel is a producer.

**Flag: VERIFIED for defendant role** — corroborated by the Stage 1 primary
docket party list (which shows Hormel Foods, LLC alongside the other named
producer-defendants and separately from the labeled plaintiff groups
"Direct Purchaser Plaintiffs," "Direct Action Plaintiffs," "Commercial and
Institutional Indirect Purchaser Plaintiffs") plus independent secondary
case-tracker corroboration with no contradicting source found.

### Material fact missing from the raw file — mandatory addition for Stage 4

**Hormel won summary judgment and was dismissed from most of this
litigation** — a fact not in the raw file that changes how Hormel's
antitrust story should be framed relative to co-defendants:

- Judge John Tunheim (D. Minnesota) granted Hormel's motion for summary
  judgment. Per multiple independent trade-press accounts (meatingplace.com,
  National Hog Farmer), the court's stated basis was that **Hormel did not
  formally subscribe to the Agri Stats benchmarking-report service** that is
  central to the alleged conspiracy — Hormel is reported to have only
  accepted free Agri Stats samples in 2017, unlike its co-defendants, who
  remain in active litigation on the merits.
- The dismissal was **partial, not total**: Hormel Foods, LLC was dismissed
  "without prejudice" as to most claims but **the consumer indirect
  purchaser plaintiffs' complaint against Hormel continued**.
- This is **not contradicted** by the raw file's ~$11M settlement figure —
  both are true simultaneously. Per the settlement-administration site
  (porkantitrustlitigation.com, hosting a "Declaration ... in Support of
  Settlement with Hormel" — a court-adjacent document, though its PDF text
  could not be extracted in this pass), Hormel separately reached settlements
  with the Direct Purchaser Plaintiffs (**$4,856,000** — matches the raw
  file's $4.8M wholesaler/direct-purchaser figure almost exactly) and other
  purchaser tiers, apparently to fully close out the litigation rather than
  litigate the surviving indirect-purchaser claim or risk appeal.
- **Basis: UNVERIFIED** (search-engine synthesis of trade press, not an
  independently read court order) **but corroborated across multiple
  independent, non-advocacy outlets with no contradicting account found.**
- **Mandatory instruction to Stage 4:** Do not write Hormel's antitrust
  posture as equivalent to Smithfield's. If used, the accurate framing is:
  "Hormel was a named defendant in pork price-fixing litigation; a federal
  judge later granted Hormel summary judgment on most claims, citing its
  limited participation in the data-sharing service central to the
  allegations, while related claims by consumers continued; Hormel also
  paid roughly $11 million combined across settlements with several
  purchaser groups without admitting fault." Given the UNVERIFIED flag on
  the summary-judgment specifics, the flag→action default is to hedge or
  omit the summary-judgment detail unless the founder wants the nuance
  surfaced — but it must not be silently dropped in a way that leaves
  Hormel looking equally culpable to Smithfield, since that would overstate
  a claim the record does not support.

### The ~$11M settlement figure itself

- **UPHELD, and now more strongly corroborated.** Independent WebSearch
  found the same $2.4M / $4.8M / $4.4M breakdown reported by Star Tribune
  (the source Stage 2 already cited), and independently by nny360.com and
  Feedstuffs — three independent secondary outlets carrying consistent
  figures, plus an official PR Newswire class-action notice ("If you
  purchased any Hormel Foods Corporation Pork product...") that is the
  court-mandated notice for this settlement. **Flag stays UNVERIFIED** per
  the strict primary-source bar (the underlying court order/declaration text
  was not independently read — the Schachter declaration PDF was located but
  could not be extracted in this pass), but this is now well past
  single-source corroboration and safe to use with the standard settlement
  hedge ("agreed to pay... without admitting fault").

## 1. GAP (Global Animal Partnership) step level

- **Claim:** Hormel/Applegate not listed on GAP's Manufacturers directory
  page; Applegate's own site claims GAP Step 4 for its cattle, corroborated
  by one 2019 trade-press piece.
  **UPHELD, with one added data point.** Independently fetched GAP's own
  dedicated page at
  `globalanimalpartnership.org/portfolio/applegate-farms/` — **this page
  exists** (confirming GAP does carry an Applegate portfolio entry) but its
  content is a bare logo and a 2017 date with no step-level text, so it
  neither confirms nor contradicts "Step 4." Separately, independent
  WebSearch surfaced a **second** trade-press outlet — Supermarket
  Perimeter, "Applegate hot dogs GAP-certified" — describing the GAP
  Animal Welfare Certified logo appearing specifically on "The Great
  Organic Uncured Beef Hot Dog" at Whole Foods, plus FoodPrint.org (an
  independent food-label explainer site, not Applegate or GAP) describing
  GAP Step 4 for beef generically as involving pasture-based systems.
  **Net: the GAP-certification claim for Applegate's beef hot dogs is now
  corroborated by two independent secondary sources beyond Applegate's own
  site (MEAT+POULTRY 2019 + Supermarket Perimeter), which is stronger than
  the raw file's single-corroboration framing — but the specific "Step 4"
  designation is still not confirmed by an independent source describing it
  as applied to Applegate specifically (FoodPrint's Step 4 description is
  generic, not company-specific).** Flag stays **UNVERIFIED** for "Step 4"
  exactly, but the underlying "GAP-certified" claim (without the step
  number) is safe to use with a source-hedge. Do not write as "not GAP
  certified" for Applegate — that would contradict the corroborated
  evidence.
- **Conclusion for Stage 4:** No GAP relationship confirmed for
  Hormel-branded (non-Applegate) products — uphold as NOT CHECKED
  exhaustively, do not write as absent.

## 3. Gestation-crate status (pork)

- **Claim:** Per Hormel's 2024 Global Impact Report (via National Hog
  Farmer summary): 19% of hogs Prop-12-compliant, 13% in loose sow housing
  (annualized, as of June 2024); 100% of MA/CA-market hogs in group pens
  from birth.
  **UPGRADED: VERIFIED.** This fact-check independently fetched Hormel's own
  page at `hormelfoods.com/global-impact/planet/supply-chain/hogs/`, which
  states, in Hormel's own words: **"19% of the total hogs we were harvesting
  on an annualized basis (regardless of destination) were produced in
  California Proposition 12 compliant systems"** and **"13% were produced in
  loose sow housed systems,"** plus the 100% MA/CA group-pens-from-birth
  figure, under a section referencing a "2023/2024 Group Sow Housing
  Report," page last updated 11/19/24. This is company-disclosure directly
  confirming the claim (not a paraphrase) — meets the VERIFIED bar. Note for
  Stage 4: the page does not name a single formal downloadable report
  document — the data lives directly on Hormel's Global Impact site rather
  than in one titled PDF; cite the URL and the "as of June 2024 / page
  updated 11/19/24" dating rather than inventing a formal report title.

- **Claim:** Company-owned sow farms converted to group housing by 2018;
  ~96% of hogs purchased under contract (FY2017-era 10-K reference) from
  250+ producers.
  **UPHELD — STALE, as Stage 2 already flagged.** No current-year figure
  found to replace the ~96%/FY2017 number in this pass. Do not present as
  current.

- **Claim:** Applegate has required group sow housing (no gestation crates)

  **UPHELD — company-disclosure, UNVERIFIED for independent audit
