## Gestation-crate status — PRIMARY FOUNDER PRIORITY

This is the pork-industry equivalent of "cage-free" for eggs, and it is where the most important, headline-worthy finding in this file lives.

**Timeline (all claims below independently sourced, dates noted):**

1. **2007** — Smithfield announced a company-wide commitment to phase out individual gestation stalls ("crates") on **company-owned** sow farms over 10 years, replacing them with group housing. Secondary trade-press source. — https://www.meatpoultry.com/articles/13665-smithfield-on-schedule-for-gestation-crate-free-target — UNVERIFIED (secondary trade press, not company primary doc)
2. **~2014–2016** — Progress reported incrementally: 71.4% of company-owned-farm sows in group housing by end of 2014; 87% by a later 2016 report. — https://www.wattagnet.com/home/article/15520190/smithfield-keeps-87-percent-of-us-sows-in-group-housing-wattagnet — UNVERIFIED (secondary trade press)
3. **June 2023** — Smithfield announced it had **fulfilled its 2007 commitment**: group housing systems for pregnant sows on **all company-owned U.S. farms**, having invested a reported $360M+ in the conversion. — https://www.feedstuffs.com/agribusiness-news/smithfield-fulfills-sow-housing-commitment-on-company-owned-farms — UNVERIFIED (secondary trade press; direct WebFetch of this URL returned 403, content taken from search-engine synthesis only, not independently confirmed against the article's full text)
4. **Contract growers were only "recommended," not required**, to convert to group housing (target year 2022 cited), via a sliding-scale incentive structure; growers who don't convert keep existing contracts but are less likely to get extensions. — https://www.csrwire.com/press_releases/36856-smithfield-foods-recommends-its-contract-growers-convert-to-group-housing-for-pregnant-sows ; https://www.farmprogress.com/management/smithfield-recommends-contract-growers-shift-to-group-housing — UNVERIFIED (secondary trade press)
5. **This matters materially for scope**: per Smithfield's own recent financial disclosures (see Sourcing model below), company-owned farms account for only **~40% of hogs processed by its Fresh Pork segment as of FY2025**, headed toward ~30%. The "fulfilled commitment" language covers only the owned-farm share of the supply chain — the majority (and growing majority) of Smithfield's hog supply comes from contract growers and the open market, where group-housing conversion is a recommendation, not a requirement.

**The HSUS lawsuit and 2025 settlement — the key intentionally-misleading-claim finding:**

- **Filed:** Oct. 18, 2021, Humane Society of the United States (HSUS) v. Smithfield Foods, Inc., Superior Court of the District of Columbia. Alleged Smithfield's marketing (including its "group housing" claims and its public statement that it had eliminated gestation-crate use) violated the D.C. Consumer Protection Procedures Act. — https://www.humaneworld.org/en/blog/were-suing-worlds-largest-pork-producer ; https://worldanimalnews.com/2021/11/15/hsus-sues-worlds-largest-pork-producer-smithfield-foods-for-not-eliminating-gestational-crates-as-promised/ — UNVERIFIED (D.C. Superior Court is a state-level court; CourtListener federal search does not cover it, consistent with the SKILL.md pilot finding about DC Superior Court cases — this was found only via direct WebSearch of `"Smithfield" lawsuit`, not via CourtListener)
- **Core allegation (HSUS's own characterization, advocacy source — record as allegation, not fact):** Smithfield's "group housing" is actually a repeating cycle — sows crated ~4–6 weeks in a gestation crate after insemination, then moved to group pens for as little as ~9 weeks, then into individual farrowing crates ~4 weeks, then back to gestation crates — repeating for the sow's whole reproductive life. HSUS characterized this as "a small reduction but not elimination" of crate confinement. — https://www.farmprogress.com/farm-operations/hsus-files-lawsuit-against-smithfield-s-sow-housing-claims — ALLEGATION, advocacy source (HSUS is the plaintiff)
- **Oct. 24, 2022** — D.C. Superior Court Judge Yvonne Williams **denied Smithfield's motion to dismiss**, finding HSUS had alleged sufficient facts to support its false/misleading-advertising claims. This is an actual court ruling (procedural — allows the case to proceed, is not itself a finding of liability). — https://www.meatpoultry.com/articles/27496-smithfields-motion-to-dismiss-a-case-over-gestation-crate-use-denied ; https://www.humaneworld.org/en/blog/breaking-court-rules-hsus-lawsuit-against-pork-producer-smithfield-can-proceed — UNVERIFIED (state court record, not independently pulled from the court's own docket system in this pass; two independent trade/advocacy outlets corroborate the ruling's existence and date)
- **Jan. 28, 2025 — SETTLED.** HSUS and Smithfield resolved the litigation. Per HSUS's own settlement announcement: Smithfield **agreed to clarify its marketing/disclosures** — the company "now discloses gestation crate usage in corporate sustainability reports for investors and in consumer-facing materials," and specifically agreed to state that it **"still crates sows for 4 to 6 weeks each pregnancy cycle following insemination."** No payment/damages amount is mentioned in the settlement press release. — https://www.humaneworld.org/en/news/humane-society-united-states-and — UNVERIFIED-tier (this is the plaintiff-side org's own settlement announcement, not a court-filed settlement agreement or Smithfield's own statement; directionally corroborated by the case's well-documented procedural history above, but the exact settlement terms were not independently confirmed against a primary legal document or a Smithfield press release in this pass)

**Net finding for the card:** Smithfield's own 2025 legal settlement is the strongest available evidence that its "group housing" / "gestation-crate-free" marketing (2018-era press release language: "Smithfield Foods delivers on decade-old promise to eliminate pregnant sow stalls in U.S.") does not mean crate-free in the way a consumer would likely understand "eliminate" — sows are still crated for a portion of every reproductive cycle, by the company's own post-settlement disclosure language.
