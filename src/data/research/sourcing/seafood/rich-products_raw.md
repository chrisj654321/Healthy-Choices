## 1. Farmed vs. wild-caught — the core "decode farmed" question

**Fact:** SeaPak's own Sourcing & Sustainability page states the company
sources BOTH farmed and wild-caught seafood, and explicitly frames
aquaculture as necessary: "responsible aquaculture is a viable solution"
given seafood demand (paraphrase of page content per WebFetch summary — see
caveat below).
- Source: SeaPak, "Sourcing & Sustainability"
- URL: https://seapak.com/sustainability/
- Date accessed: 2026-07-30
- Status: **VERIFIED** that SeaPak carries both farmed and wild-caught
  lines (company-disclosure basis for the farmed/wild split itself).
- Caveat: the WebFetch summary paraphrased rather than block-quoted the
  page; treat exact wording as approximate pending a direct-quote pass in
  Stage 3.

**Fact:** SeaPak's shrimp products (the category the Jumbo Butterfly Shrimp
belongs to) are farmed, per the same sustainability page pairing "farmed
shrimp" with "wild-caught salmon" as the company's two product pillars —
i.e., shrimp is the farmed line, salmon is the wild-caught line.
- Source/URL: same as above.
- Status: **VERIFIED** as company disclosure that SeaPak shrimp = farmed
  (not wild-caught). I did NOT find a SeaPak page specifically confirming
  the Jumbo Butterfly Shrimp SKU by name — this is a category-level
  (all-SeaPak-shrimp) disclosure, not itself SKU-specific. Flag as
  category-to-SKU inference for Stage 3 to confirm against actual package
  copy if available.

**Fact — geography:** SeaPak's own sourcing page states approximately 80% of
SeaPak's products are processed at the company's Brownsville, TX facility,
with the remaining ~20% processed in Ecuador and Thailand.
- Source: same SeaPak sustainability page; corroborated in a SeafoodSource
  article referencing SeaPak's Brownsville plant.
- URL (primary): https://seapak.com/sustainability/
- URL (corroborating): https://www.seafoodsource.com/news/foodservice-retail/seapak-introduces-new-line-of-shrimp-and-salmon-meals
- Status: **VERIFIED** as company disclosure of PROCESSING location split.
  **IMPORTANT DISTINCTION — NOT VERIFIED:** "processed in Ecuador/Thailand"
  is not the same fact as "raw shrimp farmed/grown in Ecuador/Thailand." The
  pages I could load describe processing/production location, not
  necessarily the country where the shrimp were actually farmed before
  processing. I could not find a SeaPak page that states the farming
  country of origin as distinct from the processing site. This is the exact
  ambiguity the founder's brief warned about — record the fact precisely as
  "processed in," not "farmed in," until Stage 3 finds a source that

- I searched specifically for Vietnam/India sourcing (the other two large
  Southeast Asian shrimp-farming countries) and found no SeaPak-specific
  connection — only general industry context that Vietnam and India are
  large global shrimp suppliers. Status: **NOT CHECKED** as a SeaPak-specific
  question beyond Ecuador/Thailand; no evidence found either way for
  Vietnam/India ties.

**Fact — farming system:** I could NOT find any SeaPak or Rich Products
disclosure specifying open-net/open-pond vs. closed-containment/RAS farming
system for their shrimp suppliers. Category-level context found instead (not

## 3. BAP (Best Aquaculture Practices) certification

**Fact:** SeaPak's Brownsville, TX processing plant holds BAP certification
(covering shrimp and tilapia processing). This was announced via press
release when the plant first achieved certification.
- Sources: PR Newswire, "Rich Products' Texas Manufacturing Plant Receives
  'Best Aquaculture Practices' Certification"
  (https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/rich-products-texas-manufacturing-plant-receives-best-aquaculture-practices-certification-164297706.html);
  corroborating trade coverage at reliableplant.com and foodonline.com.
- Status: **UNVERIFIED as VERIFIED-tier** — this is a company press release
  and trade-press coverage of it, not a direct pull from BAP's own
  certified-facilities directory. Treat as high-confidence but not
  primary-directory-confirmed.

**Fact:** SeaPak's own sustainability page states ALL of SeaPak's
aquaculture (farmed) products come from BAP-certified sources, with
suppliers required to be "at least 2-star BAP certified or higher," roughly
80% currently sourced from 4-star (the top tier) BAP-certified suppliers,
and a stated company goal of reaching 100% 4-star sourcing. One WebSearch
result (secondary, summarizing the same SeaPak page) reported this figure
as already "100 percent... 4-star certified" — this conflicts with the
"80%, working toward 100%" figure taken directly off the page by WebFetch.
**Flag this contradiction for Stage 3** — the WebFetch pull of the actual
page is the more reliable of the two and should be preferred, but both
should be checked against the live page again before writing.
- Source (primary-ish, direct page fetch): https://seapak.com/sustainability/
- Status: **VERIFIED** that SeaPak requires minimum 2-star BAP for
  aquaculture suppliers (company-disclosure basis) — **UNVERIFIED/DISPUTED**
  on the exact current 4-star percentage (80% vs. 100%, conflicting

## 6. FDA recall — SeaPak-specific, VERIFIED-adjacent

**Fact:** Rich Products Corporation issued a nationwide voluntary recall of
"SeaPak Breaded Butterfly Shrimp – Ready to Fry," Net Wt. 22 oz., Product
Code 10302, dated 2011-09-23, due to an undeclared milk ingredient (whey) in
the shrimp coating (allergen labeling issue, not a contamination/safety
issue in the pathogen sense). Production date of concern: 7/25/2011 (Julian
date 1003 1206); Best By date: 2013-01-25.
- Sources: multiple secondary recall-aggregator sites converged on identical
  product code/dates (recallsinthenews.com, blivenlawfirm.com,
  thebaynet.com, stlamerican.com); an FDA URL was identified
  (https://www.fda.gov/Safety/Recalls/ucm581729.htm) but not directly
  fetched/confirmed live in this pass — old FDA recall URLs of this format
  are frequently retired/redirected.
- Status: **UNVERIFIED at VERIFIED-tier** (multiple independent secondary
  sources agree on identical specifics — product code, dates, allergen —
  which is high-confidence, but I did not load the FDA source page itself
  to confirm it resolves). Stage 3 should attempt to load the FDA recall
  archive/search directly for this record.
- **Product-name note:** this recall is for "SeaPak Breaded Butterfly
  Shrimp," not explicitly "Jumbo Butterfly Shrimp" (the catalog SKU in
  scope) — likely the same product family/line but not confirmed as the
  identical SKU. Flag for Stage 3 to verify whether "Jumbo" was a later
  size variant of the same recalled line or a separate product.
- No other SeaPak/Rich Products seafood recalls were found in this pass.
  Other shrimp recalls surfaced in search results (Direct Source Seafood,
  Aquastar, Sea Port Products, Bornstein Seafoods — cesium-137 and Listeria
  issues) are UNRELATED companies, not Rich Products/SeaPak.
