## Corporate cage-free commitment (pledge / target / progress)

- **Original pledge**: Walmart U.S. and Sam's Club U.S. announced April 5, 2016 a commitment "to transition to a 100 percent cage-free egg supply chain by 2025." Primary company press release located at this URL but could not be fetched in this session (connection error): https://news.walmart.com/news-archive/2016/04/05/walmart-us-announces-transition-to-cage-free-egg-supply-chain-by-2025 — wording above is drawn from secondary reporting quoting/paraphrasing the release, not a direct primary-source extraction. Source (secondary): https://www.foodlogistics.com/sustainability/news/12191358/walmart-walmart-commits-to-100-cagefree-eggs-by-2025 ; https://www.farmprogress.com/farm-business/walmart-to-transition-to-100-cage-free-eggs-by-2025
- **2019 progress (company disclosure, basis: company reporting per secondary source)**: Reported 14% progress toward the 2025 cage-free goal. Source: secondary aggregation (search-result summary; original company report not independently located in this session).
- **2023 progress (company disclosure, basis: company reporting per secondary source)**: Cage-free eggs reported at 21% of Walmart U.S. egg sales; 41% of Sam's Club egg sales reported cage-free. Source: secondary aggregation citing Walmart reporting (original document not independently located/fetched in this session).
- **2024 progress (company disclosure, basis: company reporting per secondary source)**: Reported at 27% of sales meeting the cage-free standard as of 2024, described by secondary sources as making the original 2025 100% goal "unattainable." Source: https://www.ainvest.com/news/walmart-ethical-supply-chain-crossroads-laggards-pace-cage-free-revolution-2506/ (secondary analysis piece; not a primary Walmart disclosure — flagged accordingly).
- **Target met/extended/dropped**: Per all sources reviewed, Walmart has NOT met the 2025 100% cage-free target. Secondary reporting states Walmart has not announced a new 100% target date and — notably — is reported to have stopped publishing annual cage-free progress updates (a transparency-practice fact, contrasted in sources against Costco, which is reported to have completed its own cage-free transition and to continue publishing progress). Source: https://www.ainvest.com/news/walmart-ethical-supply-chain-crossroads-laggards-pace-cage-free-revolution-2506/ ; https://www.wattagnet.com/egg/cage-free-laying-systems/article/15536685/walmart-kroger-will-not-meet-2025-cage-free-commitments
- **Cited reasons for missing target (company/secondary reporting)**: cage-free supply availability, cost/pricing pressure on consumers, and avian-influenza disruption to cage-free-certified flocks are cited across multiple secondary sources as contributing factors industry-wide (not exclusively Walmart-specific in all cases).

## Supplier identity

- **Cal-Maine Foods, Inc.** — publicly documented, via the 2018 "Marketside Organic" litigation described above, as the producer of that specific Walmart private-label organic/cage-free egg line, with a named production facility in Chase, Kansas. Secondary reporting (business-analysis sources, not a primary Walmart or Cal-Maine disclosure) additionally characterizes Cal-Maine broadly as "Walmart's private-label organic egg supplier" and states Walmart/Sam's Club historically represents roughly 28–30% of Cal-Maine's net sales; Cal-Maine is described industry-wide as the largest U.S. shell-egg producer (~20% of the domestic market) supplying conventional, cage-free, and organic product across multiple retail customers including Walmart, Costco, Kroger, and Publix. **The specific link between Cal-Maine and the "Great Value" brand (as opposed to "Marketside," which is directly documented via litigation) was not independently confirmed with a primary source in this session** — flagged as a gap. Sources: https://talkbusiness.net/2018/01/wal-mart-cal-maine-sued-for-deceptive-organic-egg-label/ ; https://thecounter.org/walmart-organic-egg-outdoor-access-fraud-lawsuit/ ; general supplier-relationship characterization (secondary business-analysis source, not independently verified): search-aggregated summary citing Cal-Maine's customer base.
