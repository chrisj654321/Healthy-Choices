## Housing systems

- Organic Valley markets its shell eggs as **"pasture-raised"** on its own product pages (per search-indexed page titles, e.g., "Organic Large Brown Eggs, Pasture Raised, Dozen" at organicvalley.coop) — flagged per brief: "pasture-raised" has **no USDA legal definition**; it is a company marketing claim layered on top of the USDA organic certification (which itself requires outdoor access, separately defined).
- Cornucopia's scorecard (organic-only line) records "hen housing is primarily fixed, practices vary" and that "outdoor access is legitimate but suppliers are not required to rotate outdoor areas" — these are Cornucopia's measured scorecard line items, not company self-disclosure.
- A specific supplying operation was identified via a 2011 Cornucopia investigation/complaint (see Enforcement below): Petaluma Egg Farm (Petaluma, CA; also doing business, per Cornucopia's reporting, as "Judy's Family Farm" and referred to in Organic Valley's own literature at the time as "Stephen Judy's Egg Farm"). Cornucopia's 2011 reporting states this operation's outdoor access consisted solely of a "sun porch"—"entirely enclosed and screened to prevent the birds from going outside." Organic Valley's general standard for family-farm members was cited in the same reporting as "a minimum of 5 square feet per bird outdoors," with an exception reportedly granted to Petaluma citing avian-influenza concerns raised by California state authorities. This is a 2011-dated finding; current (2026) housing conditions at this specific operation were not independently re-verified in this pass (see Gaps).
- Cornucopia's separate scorecard entry for "Judy's Family Farm (Petaluma Farms)" (https://www.cornucopia.org/scorecard/eggs/judys-family-farm-petaluma-farms/) scores that specific operation 0/1700, 1-star, and states: "This brand did not participate in Cornucopia's survey or participate in Cornucopia's investigation. While some information was found about this brand's practices, we could not confirm everything." That page itself does not name Organic Valley as a customer — the Organic Valley connection comes from the separate 2011 Cornucopia news article, not from this scorecard page.

## Organic standards compliance (incl. OLPS 2024)

- No Organic-Valley-specific public statement on compliance with the 2024 USDA Organic Livestock and Poultry Standards (OLPS) final rule was located in this pass.
- Background (general federal rule, not company-specific): USDA published the OLPS final rule Nov. 2, 2023 (effective/general compliance Jan. 2, 2025), which closes the "sun porch" loophole by specifying that enclosed, solid-roofed structures do not qualify as outdoor access; egg-laying and broiler operations get an extended compliance window to Jan. 2, 2029 for the rule's outdoor stocking-density and soil/vegetation requirements. Source: https://www.ams.usda.gov/rules-regulations/organic-livestock-and-poultry-standards ; https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/11/02/2023-23726/national-organic-program-nop-organic-livestock-and-poultry-standards
- This 2023/2024 rule is the direct regulatory descendant of the "sun porch" dispute Cornucopia raised against Organic Valley's Petaluma supplier in 2011 (a 2012 USDA National Organic Program policy memo — dated Jan. 31 per secondary reporting — already stated NOP's concurrence that enclosed porches don't meet the outdoor-access definition, ahead of a 2016 proposed rule that was withdrawn in 2017 and finally replaced by the 2023 final rule). Source: https://www.commondreams.org/newswire/2011/04/13/watchdog-organic-valley-herbrucks-violating-federal-organic-standards-factory ; https://www.npr.org/sections/thesalt/2017/12/15/571187112/trump-administration-moves-to-kill-rules-for-organic-eggs

## Sourcing model

**Farmer cooperative (member-owned), not a corporation** — verified via multiple sources with some numeric variance:
- Founded 1988 as "CROPP" (Coulee Region Organic Produce Pool) Cooperative; "Organic Valley" is the brand. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_Valley
- Member-farm count cited variously as: "approximately 1,500" (organicvalley.coop, per Wikipedia summary), "around 1,600 organic family farmers in 29 states" (farmers.coop FAQ page, fetched directly), and "1,800" (Wikipedia, citing a different source/date) — the exact current figure is not perfectly reconciled across sources; treat as "roughly 1,500–1,800" pending a single dated authoritative figure. Sources: https://www.farmers.coop/frequently-asked-questions/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_Valley
- Governance (per farmers.coop, primary source, fetched directly): board members "elected by the farmers from the farmer membership"; voting is one-farm-one-vote — "Regardless of the farm's size, location, products, or volume, each farm has equal voting power." Membership requires an equity investment "of 5.5% of the farm's base annual income from products sold to the cooperative." Source: https://www.farmers.coop/frequently-asked-questions/
- **Egg Pool specifics** (per farmers.coop's dedicated Egg Pool page, fetched directly): founded 1994; "around 50 organic egg producers around the nation" currently in the pool; described as the cooperative's "second-highest pool in gross sales"; minimum flock size for participation is "5,000 birds," with the exact minimum "vary[ing] by region and proximity to existing routes." Source: https://www.farmers.coop/pools/egg-pool/
- Geographic reach (per Wikipedia, secondary): member farms located in the United States, Canada, Australia, and the United Kingdom; products distributed in all 50 U.S. states plus a total of 25 countries. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_Valley
- At least one egg-supplying operation (Petaluma Egg Farm, West Coast eggs) has been described in 2011-era Cornucopia reporting as an "industrial-scale" operation, contrasted with the cooperative's smaller family-farm members — see Enforcement/Housing sections above for detail and dating caveats.
