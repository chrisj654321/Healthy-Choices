## Corporate cage-free commitment (pledge / target / progress)

- **Original pledge (2016)**: Target announced in 2016 a commitment to "transition to a 100% cage-free egg supply chain by 2025, subject to available supply." Source (Target's own corporate sustainability page, fetched directly): https://corporate.target.com/sustainability-governance/responsible-supply-chains/animal-welfare
- **Target date and outcome**: The original target date (2025) passed without the 100% goal being met, per Target's own sustainability reporting and secondary reporting. Target's own page (fetched) states a **revised target date of 2030** for 100% cage-free unit sales and SKUs.
- **Progress figures — company disclosure (basis: Target's own corporate sustainability page, fetched directly)**:
  - Target currently operates "over 600 stores with a fully cage-free egg assortment."
  - Target currently sources "over half of its eggs by unit volume from cage-free sources," described on the page as "ahead of many national grocery peers" (a comparative claim made by Target itself, not independently verified against peer data in this session).
  - Milestone: end of 2026 — 57–61% cage-free unit sales (stated range).
  - Milestone: 2030 — 100% cage-free unit sales and SKUs.
  - Target sources 100% of liquid eggs from cage-free systems (already achieved, per the same page).
  - Target expects 100% of "National Brand" eggs (i.e., non-private-label, branded eggs Target carries) from cage-free sources by end of 2026.
  - Source: https://corporate.target.com/sustainability-governance/responsible-supply-chains/animal-welfare
- **Progress figures — secondary reporting (basis: secondary reporting on Target's annual sustainability report)**: A secondary source states that "on the last page of its annual sustainability report released at the end of July [2025]," Target disclosed it would not meet the 2016-set 100% cage-free-by-2025 goal, while reporting 68% of egg revenue came from cage-free eggs as of that report, up from 57% the prior year. **Note: this 68%/57% revenue-based figure from secondary reporting does not exactly match the "over half of unit volume" figure on Target's own current corporate page — the two may reflect different metrics (revenue vs. unit volume) or different reporting periods; not reconciled in this session.** Source: https://www.startribune.com/cage-free-cage-free-eggs-target-walmart-sustainability-esg-goals-pledges/601112057
- **Cited reasons for missing target (per Target's own statements, quoted in secondary reporting)**: "The cage-free global supply is simply unable to meet demand, and consumers are seeking affordable options." Progress also stated as subject to WIC (Special Supplemental Nutrition Program for Women, Infants, and Children) compliance rules in certain states and to potential industry disruptions such as avian influenza. Source: https://www.startribune.com/cage-free-cage-free-eggs-target-walmart-sustainability-esg-goals-pledges/601112057
- **Related regulatory-access fact**: With Target's stated support, Minnesota approved cage-free eggs for WIC program eligibility in April 2025, described as expanding access for "over 99,000 recipients." Source: same secondary reporting as above (original regulatory action not independently verified against a Minnesota state source in this session).
- **Target met/extended/dropped**: NOT MET by the original 2025 deadline; extended to 2030 per Target's own current corporate page.
