## Housing systems

Per Vital Farms company website (vitalfarms.com/faqs/) and Cornucopia scorecard entries (mixed company-disclosure and third-party-assessment basis):
- **Pasture-Raised** (flagship line): Cornucopia's scorecard records the company's own stated standard as "108.9 square feet/hen brand standard (total over lifetime as layer)." IMPORTANT LEGAL NOTE per brief: "pasture-raised" carries NO USDA legal definition. However, for Vital Farms this claim is attached to a named third-party certification — Certified Humane (Humane Farm Animal Care) — which per certifiedhumane.org describes the operation as "raising laying hens in a total outdoor pasture system, with access to shelters at night," and to a GAP Step 4 rating (per secondary source). Both of those are third-party programs with their own defined (though non-USDA) standards; this is more third-party-defined backing than a bare, uncertified "pasture-raised" claim would have, though it is still not a USDA-defined term.
- **Organic**: separate "Vital Farms Organic" product line exists per Cornucopia's distinct scorecard entry for it (USDA-recognized certification, when independently confirmed via USDA Organic Integrity Database — not done in this pass).
- **"Restorative Organic"**: a further distinct product line exists per Cornucopia's separate scorecard entry, described in search snippet as using "regenerative agriculture principles" (a marketing/agricultural-practice term, not itself a USDA legal housing classification).
- Company also sells butter, hard-boiled eggs, and liquid whole eggs (non-shell-egg products; outside the housing-system scope but noted as product breadth) per Cornucopia's "Vital Farms Organic" scorecard-page description ("sold in over 22,000 stores nationwide").

Basis: mix of Cornucopia Institute (third-party investigator) scorecard data and Certified Humane's own certifying-body page; both are third-party sources, not raw company self-disclosure alone.

## Sourcing model

**Contract-farm network (aggregator model)**, per company and secondary press:
- Vital Farms operates via long-term contracts with independent family farms; described in secondary sources (logisticsnavigators.com) as a "hub-and-spoke model linking more than 375 independent family farms to centralized processing and national distribution."
- Farm count reported as growing over time: "300 in 2023" to "over 500" (2025 press) to "surpassed 600 family farms by the end of 2025" (most recent figure found). Figures from company press release / Nasdaq syndication, i.e., company self-disclosure via press release, not independently audited.
- Per secondary source (thetransfarmationproject.org, an advocacy-oriented outlet — flagged as such): contract farmers must make a "considerable financial commitment" to build barns to Vital Farms' standards, with barn construction cost cited as "up to $1.2 million," each housing "10,000 to 20,000 birds at once." This is a secondary/advocacy source's figure, not confirmed against a Vital Farms primary disclosure in this pass.
- Cornucopia's scorecard (2024 dated) independently states: "Multiple farms supply this brand, contract production with close relationship" (Ownership Structure line item).

## 10-K risk factors (public company — SEC EDGAR, primary source)

Per secondary reporting summarizing Vital Farms' most recent Form 10-K (fiscal year ended Dec. 28, 2025, filed Feb. 26, 2026) — NOTE: the underlying 10-K document itself was not directly fetched/quoted verbatim in this pass; the below is drawn from a secondary summary of the filing's content and should be verified against the primary EDGAR document before use:
- **Customer concentration**: "Customer A" (identified as UNFI, the distributor serving Whole Foods and other natural retailers) accounted for approximately 22% of FY2025 net revenue (~$167M).
- **Agricultural/disease risk**: the outdoor-pasture housing model is described as carrying "structurally greater HPAI ... exposure than caged or cage-free alternatives"; in FY2024 one Vital Farms farm reportedly experienced a direct HPAI outbreak for the first time, and nine farms in the Midwest reportedly experienced Egg Drop Syndrome, described as "a new disease risk not disclosed in prior filings."
- **Economic/supply-chain risk**: standard risk-factor language regarding shipping/distribution cost increases tied to "economic uncertainty, domestic or geopolitical tensions, wars, inflation, trade wars or tariff regimes."
