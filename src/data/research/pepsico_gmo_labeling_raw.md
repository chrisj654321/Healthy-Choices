**Result:** Defeated, 51.41% No to 48.59% Yes (certified results).
Source: Ballotpedia, "California Proposition 37, Mandatory Labeling of Genetically Engineered Food Initiative (2012)," https://ballotpedia.org/California_Proposition_37,_Mandatory_Labeling_of_Genetically_Engineered_Food_(2012) — accessed 2026-07-19.

**PepsiCo's contribution to the "No on 37" opposition (Prop-37-specific, not combined with other 2012 props):**
- **PepsiCo, Inc.: $2,430,675**
- For comparison, other opposition donors in the same table: Monsanto $8,112,069; E.I. DuPont De Nemours & Co. $5,400,000; Dow Agrisciences $2,000,000; Bayer CropScience $2,000,000; BASF Plant Science $2,000,000; Syngenta Corporation $2,000,000; Coca-Cola North America $1,653,373; Grocery Manufacturers Association $502,000.
Source: Ballotpedia, "Donations to California's 2012 ballot propositions" (data sourced from the California Secretary of State), https://ballotpedia.org/Donations_to_California%27s_2012_ballot_propositions — table current as compiled by Ballotpedia; page accessed 2026-07-19.

**Total campaign spending, Prop 37:** Opposition ("No on 37") raised more than $45.6 million (also cited as $45,600,000); combined total for the measure ($48,893,122.84) made it the #2 highest-spending ballot measure in the U.S. in 2012.

## 2. Washington Initiative 522 (2013) — mandatory GMO labeling

**Result:** Defeated, 51.09% No to 48.91% Yes (certified November 26, 2013).
Source: Ballotpedia, "Washington Mandatory Labeling of Genetically Engineered Food Measure, Initiative 522 (2013)," https://ballotpedia.org/Washington_Mandatory_Labeling_of_Genetically_Engineered_Food_Measure,_Initiative_522_(2013) — accessed 2026-07-19.

### 2a. PepsiCo's DIRECT disclosed contribution to "No on 522" (per Washington Public Disclosure Commission data, as of October 30, 2013)
- **Pepsi Co: $1,620,899**
- Other top contributors in the same PDC-sourced table: Monsanto $5,374,411; DuPont Pioneer $3,880,159; Bayer CropScience $1,091,654; Nestle USA $1,052,742.
- Total opposition ("No on 522") raised: $22,009,926.
Source: Ballotpedia, "Washington Mandatory Labeling of Genetically Engineered Food Measure, Initiative 522 (2013)" (campaign contributions section, sourced to WA Public Disclosure Commission), same URL as above.

Cross-check, independent secondary source: Michele Simon, "Junk Food Lobby Forced to Disclose Donors to its Secret 'Defense of Brand Strategic Account,'" Eat Drink Politics, October 22, 2013 — reports PepsiCo's disclosed contribution as "$1.6+ million" as of the October 18, 2013 GMA donor disclosure. URL: https://eatdrinkpolitics.com/2013/10/22/junk-food-lobby-forced-to-disclose-donors-to-its-secret-defense-of-brand-strategic-account/ — accessed 2026-07-19. This is consistent with the $1,620,899 PDC figure above.

### 2b. PepsiCo's TOTAL contribution to GMA's "Defense of Brands" account (the vehicle GMA used to route member money into "No on 522" without disclosing individual donors — this is the larger, concealed figure, separate from 2a above)
- **PepsiCo: nearly $3 million — exact figure "$2.696 million," as of 12/3/13**
- Other top-10 contributors to the same account: Nestle USA, Inc. $1.751 million; The Coca-Cola Company $1.742 million; General Mills $996,000; ConAgra $949,000; Campbell Soup $441,000; The Hershey Company $413,000; J.M. Smucker $401,000; Kellogg $369,000; Land O'Lakes $332,000.
- Context: In 2013, GMA raised over $14 million total for the Defense of Brands account (above and beyond regular member dues) and contributed $11 million of that to "No on 522," listing GMA — not the individual member companies — as the contributor of record.
Source (primary): Washington State Office of the Attorney General, press release, "AG: Grocery Manufacturers Assoc. to pay $18M, largest campaign finance penalty in US history," November 2, 2016. https://www.atg.wa.gov/news/news-releases/ag-grocery-manufacturers-assoc-pay-18m-largest-campaign-finance-penalty-us — accessed 2026-07-19.

**Why two different PepsiCo figures exist:** $1,620,899 (2a) is what was disclosed/attributed to PepsiCo by name in Washington campaign-finance filings as of Oct. 30, 2013. $2.696 million (2b) is PepsiCo's total payment into GMA's Defense of Brands account through Dec. 3, 2013 — the account GMA used specifically to shield individual companies from having their names attached to "No on 522" contributions. Both are confirmed by name against primary/PDC-linked sources; neither should be read as a duplicate of the other.

### 2c. State of Washington v. Grocery Manufacturers Association — full case chronology, all figures/dates from WA AG primary-source press releases
- **Oct. 2013:** AG Bob Ferguson filed suit against GMA in Thurston County Superior Court, alleging GMA collected over $14 million from members for the Defense of Brands account and funneled $11 million to "No on 522" without disclosing the money came from member companies (it was reported as coming from "GMA").
- **Nov. 2, 2016:** Thurston County Superior Court Judge Anne Hirsch ruled GMA intentionally violated Washington's campaign finance disclosure laws. She tripled a base $6 million penalty to a total of **$18 million** (penalty plus punitive damages), calling it, per the AG's office, "believed to constitute the largest campaign finance judgment in United States history."
  Source: https://www.atg.wa.gov/news/news-releases/ag-grocery-manufacturers-assoc-pay-18m-largest-campaign-finance-penalty-us
- **September 2018:** WA Court of Appeals upheld the trial court's finding of a violation but reversed the trebling/intent finding, which would have reduced the penalty back to the $6 million base. (Exact day in September 2018 NOT FOUND in sources reviewed — only "September of 2018" is given by the AG's office.)
- **April 16, 2020:** WA State Supreme Court ruled GMA's violations were intentional and reinstated the full **$18 million** penalty, remanding to the Court of Appeals to decide whether the penalty was constitutionally excessive.
  Source: https://www.atg.wa.gov/news/news-releases/state-supreme-court-reinstates-18m-penalty-against-grocery-manufacturer-s
- **Nov. 9, 2020:** WA Court of Appeals unanimously ruled the $18 million penalty was NOT constitutionally excessive.
  Source: https://www.atg.wa.gov/news/news-releases/court-appeals-unanimously-upholds-18m-penalty-against-grocery-manufacturer-s
- **Jan. 20, 2022:** WA State Supreme Court, 5-4, upheld the Court of Appeals' "not excessive" ruling, affirming the $18 million penalty as constitutional. (Confirmed via WebSearch summary of contemporaneous coverage — Seattle Times, KNKX, AP-syndicated wire stories dated Jan. 20, 2022; primary WA AG press release for this specific ruling date was not directly fetched in this pass — flagged for fact-checker to pull the AG's Jan. 20, 2022 release directly if a primary citation is required.)
- **March 2, 2022 — FINAL RESOLUTION:** GMA (by then renamed Consumer Brands Association) settled rather than pursue a U.S. Supreme Court appeal. Final terms: pay $6 million (the original trial-court base penalty, not the full $18 million) directly to the state, PLUS $3 million to Washington food-insecurity nonprofits (Food Lifeline and Northwest Harvest, $1.5 million each) — **$9 million total** — and issue a public apology. GMA/CBA's verbatim apology, per the AG's office: "The Grocery Manufacturers Association accepts responsibility for failing to disclose donors in a timely manner in a 2013 ballot initiative and failing to register as a political committee in accordance with Washington State campaign finance disclosure requirements. We acknowledge the ruling of the Washington Supreme Court and apologize to the voters of Washington State."
  Source: https://www.atg.wa.gov/news/news-releases/grocery-manufacturers-association-apologizes-campaign-finance-violations-will-pay — accessed 2026-07-19.

**Net result: the case resolved for $9 million actually paid (half of the $18 million judgment), not the full $18 million, via the March 2022 settlement.** Both the $18 million judgment figure and the $9 million actual-payment figure are correct and sourced — they refer to different points in the same case and should not be conflated.

## 5. The federal endgame: National Bioengineered Food Disclosure Standard (2016)

**The law:** The Safe and Accurate Food Labeling Act / "National Bioengineered Food Disclosure Standard," commonly criticized by opponents as the "DARK Act" (Deny/Denying Americans the Right to Know), enacted as Public Law 114-216. It passed the House 230-45 (July 2015) and was signed by President Obama on July 29, 2016. It preempted state-level mandatory GMO labeling laws (including Vermont's, which had already taken effect) with a national standard permitting text, symbol, or electronic/digital disclosure (e.g., a QR code) in place of on-package text.
Source: Wikipedia, "Public Law 114-216," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Law_114-216 (background/dates cross-checked against Civil Eats, "5 Things To Know About the 'DARK Act,'" July 20, 2015, https://civileats.com/2015/07/20/5-things-to-know-about-the-dark-act/) — accessed 2026-07-19.

**GMA's and PepsiCo's role — lobbying spend, not a specific PepsiCo dollar figure for this federal push specifically (see caveat below):**
- The Grocery Manufacturers Association filed disclosures reporting **$10.5 million in lobbying expenditures in 2015** covering the anti-labeling fight and other GMA legislative priorities. Of that, EWG's analysis found GMA had, since January 2014, hired 34 lobbyists and spent **$2.8 million** on lobbying that went exclusively to advocate anti-GMO-labeling legislation.
- The six food companies that spent the most in 2015 on lobbying that touched anti-GMO-labeling legislation (among other issues) were **Coca-Cola, PepsiCo, Kellogg's, Kraft Heinz Co., Land O'Lakes and General Mills** — combined, these six reported **$20.6 million** in 2015 K Street lobbying spend on this and other issues, and **$47.9 million since 2013**. EWG's methodology counts any lobbying disclosure filing that mentioned GMO-labeling legislation among the topics lobbied, alongside other issues — it does NOT isolate a PepsiCo-only, GMO-labeling-only dollar figure.
- Industry-wide (food, farm, biotech companies and trade associations), 2015 lobbying spend that mentioned GMO labeling totaled **$101.4 million**; since 2013, the cumulative total was **$192.8 million**. Separately, EWG estimated food/biotech interests spent nearly **$200 million** opposing state GMO-labeling ballot initiatives (Props/Measures/Initiative above) — combined with lobbying, close to **$400 million** in labeling-opponent spending overall (this last combined figure explicitly excludes state-legislature lobbying, per EWG's own caveat).
- **PepsiCo's own, isolated dollar figure for lobbying specifically on the 2016 federal bill: NOT FOUND** in sources reviewed — only the combined 6-company and GMA-only figures above are available at this level of specificity.
Source: Environmental Working Group, "Food Lobby Spends $101 Million in 2015 to Avert GMO Labeling," February 25, 2016, https://www.ewg.org/research/food-lobby-spends-101-million-2015-avert-gmo-labeling — accessed 2026-07-19.

**GMA's support once the standard's final rule was published:**
- GMA SVP and General Counsel Karin Moore, on the USDA's December 2018 final rule: "Consumers demand transparency, period... the USDA made a sound decision to empower industry to give them more information about the products they consume, how they were made and where they come from." And: "Disclosure is imperative to increasing transparency, educating consumers and building trust of brands, the food industry and government. We are pleased that the USDA has now provided a structure for our companies to share this information voluntarily, building a foundation for government to more quickly respond to innovation in food and agriculture in the future."
Source: Progressive Grocer, "Industry Backs Final Rule on Single Bioengineered Food Disclosure Standard," December 20, 2018, https://progressivegrocer.com/industry-backs-final-rule-single-bioengineered-food-disclosure-standard — accessed 2026-07-19.

**PepsiCo's membership in GMA at the time of the 2016 law:** PepsiCo was a GMA member through at least 2018 — a PepsiCo executive (Vivek Sankaran, then President and COO of Frito-Lay N.A.) was named as part of GMA's board-level search committee for a new CEO in 2018. Exact confirmation of PepsiCo's GMA membership status specifically in 2016 (year of the law's passage): NOT independently re-confirmed in this pass beyond the 2018 data point above and PepsiCo's clear presence as a top Defense-of-Brands-account contributor in 2013 (Section 2b). ## 6. GMA → Consumer Brands Association rebrand (2020) and PepsiCo's current stance

**Rebrand:** GMA announced on September 26, 2019 that it would change its name to the Consumer Brands Association in 2020. The move followed a two-year exodus of major members — Campbell, Kraft Heinz, Nestle, Hershey, Unilever, and others (Dean Foods, Mars, Tyson Foods, Cargill, DowDuPont per other coverage) — with disputes over how and when to disclose GMOs cited among the "fiercely debated" issues driving departures, per GMA's own account to CNBC.
Source: CNBC, "Grocery Manufacturers Association rebrands itself, sets new agenda," September 25-26, 2019, https://www.cnbc.com/2019/09/25/grocery-manufacturers-association-rebrands-itself-sets-new-agenda.html — accessed 2026-07-19.

**PepsiCo did NOT leave — it remained a member and sits on the successor group's board.** Per WebSearch of Consumer Brands Association's published membership list and PepsiCo's own 2023 corporate political-spending disclosure: PepsiCo, Inc. is listed as a Consumer Brands Association member with board-level representation (an asterisk on the membership list denotes Board of Directors status), and PepsiCo's "2023 Trade Associations and Policy Groups" disclosure lists Consumer Brands Association at "16%" (the disclosed share of PepsiCo's dues payment attributable to lobbying activity, per PepsiCo's standard disclosure format for such filings).
Sources: Consumer Brands Association, "Membership List," https://consumerbrandsassociation.org/membership-benefits/membership-list/; PepsiCo, "2023 Trade Associations and Policy Groups" (PDF), https://www.pepsico.com/docs/default-source/sustainability-and-esg-topics/pepsico-2023-trade-associations-and-policy-groups.pdf — both accessed via search 2026-07-19. NOTE: the PepsiCo PDF was not independently opened and read verbatim in this pass (direct fetch attempts failed/returned no renderable text); the "16%" figure and membership/board claims above are as reported by web-search summarization of that document, not confirmed by direct primary-source reading. Flagged for fact-checker to open the PDF directly and confirm the exact wording/figure before this is used in any published claim.

**PepsiCo's current (2025) on-record stance on bioengineered/GMO ingredients and labeling**, per PepsiCo's own corporate ESG disclosure, last updated October 3, 2025:
- "We support the use of innovative technologies when they are proven safe for both humans and the environment. The use of bioengineered food and ingredients is safe."
- "It is PepsiCo's policy to comply with all relevant regulatory requirements where it operates" regarding bioengineered-ingredient labeling.
- "We believe that labeling standards should be clear and consistent so that consumers can better understand the safety, prevalence and benefits of bioengineered ingredients and can make informed choices for themselves and their families."
- PepsiCo states some of its business units/brands choose not to use bioengineered ingredients regardless of local regulatory approval, citing as examples: Stacy's® Pita Chips, Quaker® Organic Oatmeal, Simply® brand snacks, and Off the Eaten Path® product lines "in certain markets" (third-party non-GMO verified).
- Per a separate WebSearch-sourced claim (not independently opened/verified in this pass): PepsiCo, along with Coca-Cola, ConAgra, and Utz, has chosen to use electronic/digital disclosure (e.g., QR code) rather than on-package text labels to comply with the National Bioengineered Food Disclosure Standard — source cited as Center for Science in the Public Interest, "Who's labeling what: Examining how companies are disclosing bioengineered ingredients," https://www.cspi.org/blog/whos-labeling-what-examining-how-companies-are-disclosing-bioengineered-ingredients — NOT independently fetched/confirmed verbatim in this pass; flagged for fact-checker follow-up.
Source (primary, directly read): PepsiCo, "Bioengineered food and ingredients" (ESG Topics), https://www.pepsico.com/en/esg-topics/bioengineered-food-and-ingredients — page states "Last updated October 3, 2025"; fetched and read directly 2026-07-19.
