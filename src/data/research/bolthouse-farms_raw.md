## Issues (recalls, settlements, litigation)

1. **October 15, 2025 — California Prop 65 settlement over cadmium (ADJUDICATED settlement).** AG Number 2025-00655. 60-day notice filed 2025-02-28 by Environmental Health Advocates, Inc. against Wm. Bolthouse Farms, Inc. and Ralphs Grocery Company, alleging cadmium in "Bolthouse Farms Immunity Juice – Carrot, Turmeric, Ginger" (California AG's official 60-day-notice record names the product this way, not "1915 Organic Immunity Juice" — flagging this discrepancy from the brief for review). Settlement terms: cessation of sales and point-of-sale warnings, non-contingent civil penalty of $3,000.00, plus attorney's fees and costs of $24,500.00, for a **total of $27,500.00**. This is a signed settlement agreement, i.e., adjudicated/resolved, not merely alleged. Sources: [CA OAG 60-Day Notice 2025-00655](https://oag.ca.gov/node/599294); [settlement agreement PDF](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/prop65/settlements/2025-00655S6233.pdf) (PDF text could not be cleanly extracted by automated fetch in this pass; figures above are corroborated by the OAG notice page and consistent secondary search summaries).

2. **June 2016 — protein drink recall (spoilage/illness).** Approx. 3.8 million bottles distributed in the US (plus approx. 277,000 in Canada) of Bolthouse Farms protein shakes recalled for possible spoilage — lumpy texture, off taste/odor. Recall followed consumer complaints including illness reports; exact cause reported as under investigation at the time. Affected: multiple Protein PLUS shake varieties (best-by dates between June 20 and Sept. 18, 2016) and select Mocha Cappuccino Perfectly Protein products. Sources: [Food Engineering](https://www.foodengineeringmag.com/articles/95907-bolthouse-farms-recalls-millions-of-protein-drinks), 2016-06-23; [Food Safety News](https://www.foodsafetynews.com/2016/06/127981/).

3. **October–November 2012 — Salmonella carrot chip recall (STALE, per brief).** Voluntary recall of a limited quantity of 16-oz Carrot Chips after a routine North Carolina state sampling event on 2012-10-22 detected possible Salmonella in one tested bag; company notified 2012-10-31. Approx. 5,600 cases recalled, sold under Bolthouse Farms, Safeway Farms, and Farm Stand labels, best-if-used-by dates 11/12/2012 and 11/13/2012. No illnesses reported. Sources: [Campbell's/Bolthouse press release](https://www.thecampbellscompany.com/newsroom/press-releases/bolthouse-farms-voluntarily-recalls-limited-quantity-of-16-ounce-carrot-chips/); [KVPR](https://www.kvpr.org/health/2012-11-02/bolthouse-farms-carrot-chips-recalled-over-salmonella-risk).

4. **PFAS class-action litigation — ALLEGED, not adjudicated.** Multiple federal suits allege Bolthouse Farms smoothies (Green Goodness, Blue Goodness, Amazing Mango, Mango Cherry C-Boost, Berry Superfood Boost) marketed as "100% Fruit Juice"/"100% Fruit and Vegetable Juice" contain PFAS ("forever chemicals"); one cited independent test allegedly found PFAS in Green Goodness at ~95x the EPA lifetime health advisory level for drinking water (allegation, not a regulatory finding).
   - **Tate et al. v. Wm. Bolthouse Farms, Inc.**, Case No. 1:23-cv-01038, filed 2023-07-12, U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of California (Bakersfield). Plaintiffs Lakema Tate and Richard Ortiz; counsel Milberg Coleman Bryson Phillips Grossman and Sultzer Law Group. Class-action complaint, jury trial demanded. Source: [ClassAction.org complaint PDF](https://www.classaction.org/media/tate-et-al-v-wm-bolthouse-farms-inc.pdf); [Law.com coverage](https://www.law.com/therecorder/2023/07/13/customers-sue-bolthouse-farms-claiming-its-smoothies-have-harmful-chemicals/), 2023-07-13.
   - **Smith v. Wm. Bolthouse Farms, Inc.**, Case No. 2:23-cv-00373 — related PFAS smoothie claim. Source: [ClassAction.org complaint PDF](https://www.classaction.org/media/smith-v-wm-bolthouse-farms-inc.pdf).
   - A ClassAction.org investigation-summary page (2026) states a "previously filed class action lawsuit was dismissed," without specifying which one or the grounds; not independently confirmed against a docket in this pass.
   - Company response: reported in Law360 coverage that Bolthouse argued the PFAS suit "stretches the bounds of liability" (company's litigation position, not a finding).
