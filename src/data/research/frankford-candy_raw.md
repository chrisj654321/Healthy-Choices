## Issues

### December 2023 lawsuit — Candy Land Gummy Dots choking injury (ALLEGED — this entire section is a civil complaint's claims, not a finding of fact)

- Incident date: Dec. 13, 2022. A then-3-year-old California child, Amelie Paredes Sotelo, choked on a "Candy Land Gummy Dots" candy (Frankford-manufactured, Hasbro-licensed). A home Ring camera reportedly captured the choking incident. Source: [WWNY/Gray Media, Jan. 5 2024](https://www.wwnytv.com/2024/01/05/vibrant-toddler-left-quadriplegic-after-choking-piece-candy-lawsuit-says/); [Philadelphia Inquirer, Dec. 28 2023](https://www.inquirer.com/news/lawsuit-choking-frankford-candy-land-gummy-dots-20231228.html).
- Alleged injuries: permanent brain damage from prolonged oxygen deprivation, spastic quadriplegia, inability to speak, neurogenic bladder (requires daily catheterization) and neurogenic bowel (requires daily suppositories); child requires a feeding tube. Source: same Inquirer and WWNY articles above; also [Newsweek, Jan. 2024](https://www.newsweek.com/childrens-candy-leaves-toddler-brain-damaged-paralyzed-lawsuit-1858411).
- Lawsuit filed: Dec. 28, 2023 (per Inquirer publication date), in the Philadelphia County Court of Common Pleas, on behalf of the child's parents (Maria Aylin Sotelo Camacho and Francisco Paredes Rivera), by Philadelphia attorney Thomas Bosworth.
- Case/docket number: NOT FOUND in available news coverage. None of the outlets reviewed (Inquirer, Fox affiliates syndication, Law & Crime, Newsweek) published the docket number. A docket number would need to be pulled directly from the Philadelphia County Court of Common Pleas civil docket search (https://www.courts.phila.gov) to confirm.
- Named defendants: Hasbro, Inc.; Frankford Candy & Chocolate Co., Inc.; Frankford Candy LLC. Source: [Inquirer, Dec. 28 2023](https://www.inquirer.com/news/lawsuit-choking-frankford-candy-land-gummy-dots-20231228.html).
- Legal theories alleged: product liability, failure to warn, negligence, negligent infliction of emotional distress. The complaint alleges the candy was "negligently, recklessly, and defectively designed" due to "extreme stickiness and inability to adequately breakdown from introduction of human saliva," and that the product lacked a choking-hazard warning label or a recommended age range.
- Damages sought: "more than $50,000" (Pennsylvania's standard jurisdictional pleading threshold, not necessarily the actual sought amount) plus costs; jury trial requested.
- **Status: ALLEGED / UNADJUDICATED.** No verdict, settlement, or dismissal was found in this research pass — most recent coverage located is from Jan. 2024, with no confirmed 2024–2025 update on resolution. This is a pending civil claim, not a proven fact or admission of liability by Frankford Candy or Hasbro.
