## 4. VERIFIED fact drops

Rule applied: every claim below is a regulatory fact from a primary source, no health-outcome causation implied — the regulatory action speaks for itself.

16. **Title:** Legal until 2027 · _[EXPIRES Jan 15, 2027 — pull from rotation then]_
    **Body:** The FDA banned Red 3 from food in Jan 2025 — but gave companies until Jan 15, 2027 to actually pull it.
    **Source:** https://www.fda.gov/food/hfp-constituent-updates/fda-revoke-authorization-use-red-no-3-food-and-ingested-drugs
    **Verification note:** FDA order dated Jan 15, 2025; compliance deadline Jan 15, 2027 for foods (Jan 2028 for ingested drugs). Confirmed on FDA.gov.

17. **Title:** Banned, then banned again
    **Body:** The FDA pulled BVO from sodas in July 2024. It had been legal in citrus drinks for decades.
    **Source:** https://www.fda.gov/food/hfp-constituent-updates/fda-revokes-regulation-allowing-use-brominated-vegetable-oil-bvo-food
    **Verification note:** Final rule effective Aug 2, 2024; 1-year compliance window. Confirmed on FDA.gov and Federal Register.

18. **Title:** Different country, different label
    **Body:** The EU has required a warning label on 6 specific dyes since 2010: "may affect activity and attention in children." The US doesn't.
    **Source:** https://www.cspi.org/cspi-news/why-are-there-no-eu-style-food-dye-warning-labels-us ; underlying rule: EU Regulation 1333/2008
    **Verification note:** The "Southampton Six" (Tartrazine E102, Quinoline Yellow E104, Sunset Yellow E110, Carmoisine E122, Ponceau 4R E124, Allura Red E129) require this exact warning text under EU law since July 2010. No equivalent US label requirement. Confirmed via CSPI reporting citing the EU regulation.

19. **Title:** The whitener Europe doesn't allow
    **Body:** The EU banned titanium dioxide as a food additive in Feb 2022. It's still common in US candy and gum.
    **Source:** https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/sante/items/732079/en
    **Verification note:** EU Regulation 2022/63 withdrew authorization for E171, effective Feb 7, 2022 (6-month sell-through). Confirmed on European Commission site.

20. **Title:** One state said enough
    **Body:** California is banning Red 3, potassium bromate, BVO, and propylparaben from food by Jan 2027. The rest of the US hasn't.
    **Source:** https://www.gov.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/AB-418-Signing.pdf
    **Verification note:** California Food Safety Act (AB 418), signed Oct 2023, effective Jan 1, 2027. All four named additives confirmed in the bill text and signing summary.

21. **Title:** A "possible carcinogen" in your bread
    **Body:** IARC classifies potassium bromate as a Group 2B possible human carcinogen. It's still allowed in US flour, up to 50 ppm.
    **Source:** https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK402079/ (IARC Monograph Vol. 73, 1999)
    **Verification note:** IARC classification confirmed (Group 2B, based on animal carcinogenicity data, inadequate human evidence — stating the classification only, no causation claim). FDA allowance level (50 ppm flour, 75 ppm whole wheat) confirmed via legal/regulatory summaries citing 21 CFR 137.

22. **Title:** Two friendly words, a locked room
    **Body:** "Natural flavor" can legally contain many compounds under FDA rules — and brands don't have to list them.
    **Source:** https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-101/subpart-B/section-101.22
    **Verification note:** 21 CFR 101.22 defines "natural flavor" by source material, not a simplicity limit; referenced lists (21 CFR 172.510 etc.) name roughly 130+ approved flavoring substances that can be blended under one label term. Confirmed on eCFR — no fixed "number of ingredients" claimed, phrasing kept general per the rule text.

23. **Title:** Same aisle, different rulebook
    **Body:** Titanium dioxide, Red 3, potassium bromate: all restricted or banned in the EU. All still legal in US food today.
    **Source:** compiled from sources #16, #19, #20/#21 above (FDA.gov, European Commission)
    **Verification note:** Cross-reference of individually-verified facts. _Fable review 2026-07-04: removed BVO from this line — BVO's US compliance window ended Aug 2, 2025, so "still legal in US food" was false for BVO as of 2026. Remaining three are accurate: Red 3 legal until Jan 2027 (line expires then, same as #16), potassium bromate and titanium dioxide still permitted federally._

24. **Title:** The deadline nobody advertises
    **Body:** FDA and HHS asked companies in April 2025 to drop Red 3 faster than the legal 2027 deadline.
    **Source:** (per FDA Red 3 order + HHS April 2025 announcement, corroborated in reporting — see #16 verification note)
    **Verification note:** HHS/FDA "phase out synthetic dyes" announcement April 22, 2025 confirmed in reporting corroborating the FDA order page. Keep as general regulatory-timeline statement; do not name specific companies without individual confirmation.

## Rejected / unverifiable — cut, not softened

- **"Skittles dropped titanium dioxide in the US after legal pressure"** — true per prior X-post batch and widely reported (2016 lawsuit + reformulation), but I could not re-verify a primary/regulatory source in this pass within scope (regulatory facts only, per this brief's rule — that's a corporate reformulation story, not an FDA/EU action). Cut from the verified-facts section; fine to keep in social copy where the standard is "documented reporting," not primary regulatory source.
- **Any claim naming a specific product/brand as currently containing Red 3, BVO, potassium bromate, or titanium dioxide** — formulations change constantly (per the X-post batch's own accuracy note: "companies reformulate"). Cut from notification copy entirely since these are evergreen, recurring pushes (not a dated one-time post) — a "still contains X" claim could go stale and become false while the notification is still firing. Kept the fact drops ingredient/regulation-focused, not brand-accusation-focused, for this reason.
- **4-MEI / caramel color / Prop 65 claim (used in X post #12)** — did not re-verify in this pass; if wanted for notifications later, needs its own primary-source check (Prop 65 listing status) before reuse here.
- **Any numeric sugar/ingredient-count comparison ("one has 6g sugar, one has 19g")** — real per-product numbers require in-app data lookup, not marketing copy; excluded from this bank since notification copy fires at the OS level without guaranteed product context.
- **Streak language ("day 7 of your streak!," "don't break your streak")** — deliberately excluded per brief: positive-progress must never guilt or gamify loss-aversion.
