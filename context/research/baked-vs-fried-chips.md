# Baked vs. fried vs. popped snacks — evidence review (Chad, 2026-08-19)

**Status:** Research complete, reviewed. Recommendation adopted into the scorer (Option C).

## Conclusion

"Baked," "popped," and "air-puffed" can mean less absorbed oil than deep-frying, but they do NOT automatically make a snack healthy. The strongest predictors remain the ingredient base, fiber and protein, calorie density, sodium, added sugar, oil quantity, and degree of formulation.

## Key evidence

- **Oil/calories:** Deep-frying replaces lost water with oil (potato crisps ~20–43% fat by weight). Baked chips are generally lower in fat than fried, but many remain calorie-dense (water removed, oil still added). "Baked" should earn credit only from the actual Nutrition Facts, not the word. (PubMed 22171702; PMC11961412; PubMed 37128034)
- **Acrylamide:** Baking is NOT an exemption — FDA says acrylamide forms in frying, roasting, AND baking. Levels vary by product/lot; do not rank brands without analytical data. In one experiment baking at 170°C produced MORE acrylamide than frying. (FDA acrylamide assessment; Hacettepe study)
- **Sodium:** Cooking method does not reduce sodium — salt is applied separately. Compare the label (≤5% DV low, ≥20% high). (FDA sodium guidance)
- **Glycemic:** A refined potato/rice/corn/tapioca/wheat-starch base stays rapidly digestible whether fried, baked, or puffed; extrusion can raise glycemic index. Whole grains beat refined for glucose/insulin response — the food matrix matters more than the cooking claim. (PubMed 36010538; PubMed 34930065)
- **Popcorn's one real advantage:** whole intact kernels — 100 cal of popcorn gave more satiety and lower total intake than 150 cal of potato chips. Simple popcorn is a different case from refined-starch puffs. (PubMed 22978828)
- **NOVA:** Most extruded/molded/flavored puffs and veggie straws are Group 4 (refined starches, protein isolates, maltodextrin, flavors, colors, emulsifiers). Simple kernel popcorn (corn + oil + salt) may be Group 3. NIH crossover trial: ultra-processed diet drove +508 cal/day and weight gain vs. unprocessed, matched for macros. (PMC12129239; PubMed 31105044)

## "Veggie" chips — vegetables or marketing? (ingredient-order decides)

- **Starch-first, no veggie credit:** Sensible Portions Veggie Straws (potato starch/flour + corn starch first), Popchips (potato + rice flour + starch), Pirate's Booty (puffed rice/corn), Stacy's Pita Chips (enriched wheat flour first, 300mg sodium).
- **Legume/whole-grain-forward, real but limited improvement:** Harvest Snaps (green peas first, 4g fiber/5g protein), Hippeas (chickpea flour first but also rice flour/maltodextrin/sugar), Off The Eaten Path (rice flour first, then legumes — mixed), Way Better (whole-grain corn + sprouted seeds).
- **Simple popcorn = strongest structure:** LesserEvil / Angie's plain popcorn (popcorn, oil, salt). But Angie's kettle corn has 8g added sugar — whole-grain status real, sweetened formulation still penalized.

## Scorer recommendation — Option C (adopted)

- Keep the **deep-fried cap at 50**.
- Apply a **65 cap** to industrial baked/popped/air-puffed chip analogues that are starch-first, extruded/molded, or carry NOVA markers (maltodextrin, modified starch, flavors, colors, protein isolates).
- **No credit** for the words "baked," "popped," "air-puffed," "veggie," or "sprouted."
- **Exempt simple whole-kernel popcorn** (popcorn + oil + salt + ordinary seasonings) from the process cap — score calories/oil/sat-fat/sodium/added-sugar normally (so sweet kettle corn still loses points).
- Legume-first snacks (Harvest Snaps) may approach 65 on real fiber/protein, but not treated like whole vegetables.
- No vegetable credit when potato/rice/corn/tapioca starch precedes vegetable paste/powder.
- Do NOT score acrylamide at brand level without analytical data.

65 is a policy judgment, not a clinical threshold — a real but limited advantage over deep-frying, while stopping refined-starch puffs from scoring like minimally processed foods.
