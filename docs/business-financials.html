🖥️ What Is a "Server" and Do You Even Need One?

The simple explanation

A server is just a computer that runs 24/7 somewhere in the world and responds when your app sends it a request. Think of it like a store's back room — users see the app (the storefront), but the server is where the data lives and the heavy lifting happens.

✅ Great news: everything you need is on free tiers

Your architecture keeps server costs at $0 for launch:

• Scan history, preferences, and scoring logic all live on the user's device
• Product lookups hit the Open Food Facts API — free and unlimited for reasonable use
• Supabase handles accounts/login and the account-deletion function — free tier covers ~10K+ users
• RevenueCat manages subscriptions — free until $2,500/mo revenue

This means you can launch, get your first 500+ paying users, and spend near $0 on infrastructure.

⚠️ When server costs WILL appear (future features)

• Cloud scan history backup — syncing history across devices (today it's local-only)
• Real-time product database updates — your own catalog instead of Open Food Facts
• Push notifications — alerting users to ingredient recalls, etc.
• Supabase past free tier — $25/mo Pro plan, only at significant scale

None of these are needed for launch. Build them when you have revenue to fund them.

🍎 App Store Fees — What Apple & Google Take

Apple App Store

Standard cut 30% Large developers
Small Business Program 15% Under $1M/year

You automatically qualify for the 15% rate as a new developer. Apple takes this cut from every subscription payment, every month, forever — directly before the money reaches you.

Google Play Store

Standard cut 30% Large developers
Reduced rate 15% First $1M/year

Same deal as Apple. You get 15% rate automatically until you hit $1M in annual revenue — which is a great problem to have.

Bottom line: plan on keeping 85 cents of every dollar

At your scale as a new indie developer, both stores take 15%. All the breakeven math below uses 15%.

🗓️ Realistic Path to Profit

Month 1–2: Launch ($8/mo cost)

You only pay $8.25/mo (Apple developer, amortized). Get even 4 subscribers at $2.99 and you're already profitable. Your only real upfront costs are $99 (Apple) + $25 (Google) = $124 total.

Month 3–6: Early growth (still ~$8/mo)

RevenueCat is free until you hit $2,500 MRR. At $2.99/mo that's 833 paying subscribers before you owe RevenueCat anything. You'll likely be well under that for months.

Month 6–12: Add backend when needed (~$30–50/mo)

Only add server infrastructure when users start asking for account backup or cross-device sync. Supabase free tier handles thousands of users. You add this cost AFTER you have the revenue to support it.

Year 2+: Scale costs are % of revenue, not fixed

RevenueCat goes to 1% of revenue above $2,500 MRR. Server costs scale gradually. By the time costs go up meaningfully, your revenue will be much higher.

💡 Pricing Recommendation

The best risk-free strategy: start at $3.99/mo + $27.99/yr

• $3.99/month is still 60% cheaper than Exposr and 50% cheaper than Oasis — you look like a steal
• $27.99/year (~$2.33/mo) gives users a compelling reason to commit annually — better for your cashflow
• You only need 13 paying subscribers to cover your max $50/mo costs
• At 200 subscribers you're making ~$580/mo profit — enough to fund any server infrastructure you need
• You can always discount or run promos — it's much harder to raise prices later
